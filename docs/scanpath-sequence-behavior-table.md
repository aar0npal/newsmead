# Published scanpath sequences and reading-behavior observations

**Scope (2026-09-24).** This is the sequence register for the [current NewsMead word-and-line scanpath proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md). It covers the named movement classes, line behaviors, and longer path families in the primary studies used for that proposal. It is not an exhaustive review of all reading research or a validated NewsMead detector. The arrow examples below **illustrate each paper's rule**; they are not copied participant traces. `w` is a word in document order, `L` is a physical text line, and `S` is a horizontally progressive reading segment. Every transition presumes that both endpoints were assigned correctly and that the observations are continuous.

The column headed **Reading-behavior observation** describes eye movement or the authors' reported interpretation. A movement alone cannot establish comprehension, confusion, intentional skipping, or a need for an aid. The papers used laboratory eye trackers and tasks that differ from NewsMead's front-camera, on-phone news reading. See the [study ledger](scanpath-scaffolding-evidence.md) for hardware, populations, outcomes, and transfer limits.

For the exact reference behind each paper's labels—manual transition annotation, known text geometry, instructed task, sentence manipulation, or exploratory clustering—see the [ground-truth audit](scanpath-pattern-ground-truth-audit.md). It marks which NewsMead arrow examples have no independently validated behavioral meaning.

## Movement codes, transition classes, and recognition rules

| Primary study and unit | Sequence or recognition rule (schematic) | Published label | Reading-behavior observation | Evidence/interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Olivier et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/15/4/28), consecutive **word-assigned fixations** | `w_i → w_(i+2 or later)` | `Fwd+` | Forward landing more than one word ahead. | A word between landings was not fixated in this transition; it may still have been processed. No difficulty or speed diagnosis from one code. |
| Olivier et al. 2022, word fixations | `w_i → w_(i+1)` | `Fwd` | Progression to the next word. | A single step does not identify a reading phase. |
| Olivier et al. 2022, word fixations | `w_i → w_i` | `Rfx` | Another fixation on the **same word**. | This is a refixation, not a later revisit after leaving the word, a long line dwell, or proof of word difficulty. |
| Olivier et al. 2022, word fixations | `w_i → w_(i-1)` | `Bwd` | Landing on the immediately previous word. | A backward movement may support useful rereading. |
| Olivier et al. 2022, word fixations | `w_i → w_(i-2 or earlier)` | `Bwd-` | Landing more than one word backward. | Distance does not reveal the reader's reason for returning. |
| [Yamaya et al. 2017](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/25/0/25_100/_pdf), **segment transitions** | End of line `L_i` → start of next line `L_(i+1)`, right-to-left on screen | Return Sweep | Ordinary move to the next text line. | 940/1,606 labeled transitions; classifier F1 0.91. Screen-left movement alone is insufficient. |
| Yamaya et al. 2017, segments | `S_a → S_b` farther right on the **same line** | Progressing | Continued reading of the next area after the segment detector split one line. | 99/1,606; F1 0.66. Authors call it largely a segmentation artifact, not a distinct cognitive state. |
| Yamaya et al. 2017, segments | Later position → earlier position on the **same line** | Short Re-reading | Same-line rereading or correction after an overshooting saccade. | 133/1,606; F1 0.47. The authors mention possible non-understanding, but the movement itself does not establish it. |
| Yamaya et al. 2017, segments | Current line → an **earlier line**, possibly continuing backward | Long Re-reading | Return to a previously read sentence, phrase, or word across lines. | 180/1,606; F1 0.81. Reason and benefit of return were not independently identified. |
| Yamaya et al. 2017, segments | Current segment → a farther target before the current line is fully traversed | Skipping | A target/segment was bypassed under the paper's layout and annotation rule. | 52/1,606; F1 0.31. It cannot prove unseen text was unread or intentionally ignored. |
| Yamaya et al. 2017, segments | After a Long Re-reading or Skipping segment → the prior latest reading point | Resuming | Return to the place reached before that detour. | 202/1,606; F1 0.53. Requires path history, not a single movement. |
| [Wang et al. 2024, GazePrompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772), **fixation-level line identification** | Assigned line stays `L_i` across successive fixations | Line following | Gaze remains assigned to the current line as reading proceeds. | A system recognition rule in low-vision desktop reading, not a test that progress or comprehension is normal. |
| Wang et al. 2024, line fixations | Line end → next-line start with a large horizontal return and vertical change | Line switching / return sweep | Change to the following line; a next-line cue was displayed. | In 13 low-vision adults reading aloud, line support reduced switching time; switching accuracy did not significantly improve. Detection thresholds were device/layout specific. |
| Wang et al. 2024, line fixations | `L_i → L_j` without a detected return sweep; new line ID persists for three fixations | Line jumping | System treats this as a move to a different line, including possible skip or revisit. | Authors describe a slow back-trace that their system could mislabel as a jump. No verified intent or difficulty label. |
| [Parker et al. 2019](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/31639/1/VR_Rev2_TJS_JAK.pdf), **multiline saccades** | End of `L_i` → near the start of `L_(i+1)` | Return sweep | Normal line change in multiline reading. | Studied in adults and children with a laboratory tracker; not a phone detection threshold. |
| Parker et al. 2019, saccades | Return sweep lands short of the next line's beginning → corrective leftward saccade on that next line | Undersweep and correction | Repositioning toward the intended line start. | This leftward motion can occur during normal progression, so it should not be automatically counted as rereading. |
| [Carr et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0), **line-assignment method examples** | Later word → earlier point on the **same line** | Within-line regression | Return to earlier material within a line. | Used as a natural-reading example and a simulation condition for vertical-drift algorithms; not an aid-need class. |
| Carr et al. 2022, line-assignment method examples | Current line → previously read line → return to prior place | Between-line regression | Reread earlier-line material and resume. | A difficult case for line assignment; an apparent line return may also be mapping error. |

For Olivier, the five symbols are a **word-displacement alphabet**; the authors inferred broader phases from sequences of these symbols. Their sample comprised 15 retained French-speaking adults doing a topic-relevance judgment with an EyeLink 1000. Their word assignment used a task/language-specific spatial window, not raw nearest-word landing. For Yamaya, the six labels apply to transitions between detected **segments**, not one movement between NewsMead physical-line ordinals. They were manually labeled in 33 English-document trials from 11 readers using a Tobii TX300; the reported F1 scores are classifier performance in that setting. The classifier used two preceding **and two following** transitions, so its performance does not establish a causal live detector. Carr's regression rows are line-assignment method examples, not a fitted behavior taxonomy. [Olivier source](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/8415); [Yamaya source and Table 1](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/25/0/25_100/_pdf).

## Longer path patterns and inferred reading phases

These are **patterns across multiple fixations**, not deterministic meanings of a two-item arrow. The authors' behavior names are included so the panel can see exactly where an interpretation came from.

| Primary study and unit | Observed sequence signature or pattern | Published group / phase | Authors' reading-behavior interpretation | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [von der Malsburg & Vasishth 2011](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2011.pdf), **sentence-region scanpaths** | Return to sentence beginning, then reread the whole sentence or part of it | Cluster A | Sentence rereading; this was the only main cluster significantly more frequent in the garden-path condition. | Reanalysis/confirmation is an interpretation in syntactically ambiguous Spanish sentences, not a general phone-reading diagnosis. |
| Same study | One saccade back to early sentence material, without the sustained path of A | Cluster B | Brief return to earlier material. | Was not significantly more frequent in the garden-path condition. One return does not show what information was obtained. |
| Same study | Shift back to nearby disambiguating material | Cluster C | Rechecking the critical region is a plausible interpretation. | Was not significantly more frequent in the garden-path condition; the authors discuss both oculomotor and processing explanations. |
| Same study, finer seven-dimensional exploratory clustering | D reproduces A; E rereads only the first line; F–M subdivide B mainly by landing position; N–P subdivide C by return and fixation details; Q blends B/C | Clusters D–Q | Finer variants of the three main path families. | The authors chose seven dimensions arbitrarily for comparison; these 14 cluster IDs are not 14 validated reading states. Only whole-sentence rereading remained condition-sensitive. |
| [Parshina et al. 2022](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rrq.414), **whole-sentence word scanpaths** | Mostly straight left-to-right, fast, some unfixated words, rare regressions | Fluent cluster | Typical of many monolingual Russian adults in this study. | A descriptive cluster over whole sentences; its name does not grade an individual NewsMead moment. |
| Same study | Slower pace, longer fixations, little/no word skipping, short backward movements | Intermediate cluster | More local rereading; authors suggest local processing challenges. | The suggested challenge is not diagnosed from an isolated return. |
| Same study | Slowest pace, long fixations, little/no word skipping, frequent word and whole-sentence rereading | Beginner cluster | Authors suggest broader linguistic integration challenges in some reader groups. | Exploratory Russian-language clusters across different age/language groups, not an aid trigger or universal proficiency test. |
| [Olivier et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/15/4/28), **modeled runs of word-transition codes** | Mixed ordinary forward steps and refixations across a run | Normal reading (`NR`) | Authors' interpretation of one model phase. | Inferred from a fitted hidden semi-Markov model and external features; no one arrow equals `NR`. |
| Same study | Refixations and changes of movement direction in a run | Information search (`IS`) | Authors interpret a short phase within their topic search task. | Search is task-specific; no single refixation means search. |
| Same study | Relatively high `Fwd+` probability across a run | Fast reading (`FR`) | Authors interpret a faster passage through text. | A forward jump alone cannot identify reading speed or comprehension. |
| Same study | Relatively more backward movement in a terminal run | Slow confirmation (`SC`) | Authors interpret checking near the end of topic-relevance judgment. | A return alone does not mean confirmation or difficulty. Model duration is measured in fixation steps, not one long fixation. |

The [2011 paper](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2011.pdf) reanalyzed 44 undergraduates reading Spanish sentences. The [Russian study](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rrq.414) is explicitly exploratory and treats its clusters as descriptive. [Olivier et al.](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/8415) fitted five latent states grouped into four interpreted phases in a topic-search task; their phase labels were supported with additional timing and text variables, but have not been validated for NewsMead.

## Relation to the examples in the NewsMead proposal

The following are **NewsMead operational examples**, not named sequences or validated behavioral states reported by those papers. They are useful only after reliable assignment at the stated resolution.

| NewsMead example | What it can report | Published analogue; what remains unproven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `w12 → w13 → w12` | Adjacent-word progression followed by a one-word return. | Olivier's `Fwd` then `Bwd`; reason for the return is unknown. |
| `w12 → w12` | Two consecutive fixations assigned to the same word. | Olivier's `Rfx`; it is not a later word revisit or a long fixation by itself. |
| `w12 → w15` | A landing farther ahead in observed word order. | Olivier's `Fwd+`; intervening words cannot be called unread. |
| `L4(end) → L5(start)` | Candidate normal next-line change when geometry supports it. | Yamaya/Parker return sweep and GazePrompt line switching; `L4 → L5` without end/start geometry is merely adjacent-line progression. |
| `L5 → L4 → L5` or `L4 → L5 → L4 → L5` | Repeated assigned visits to lines. | Related to multiline rereading and resuming, but **no cited paper names or validates these exact cycles as difficulty classes**. |
| `L4` with long observed residence or slow within-line word progress | Time or pace descriptor, if coverage and text amount are known. | GazePrompt's word hesitation rule concerns one-pass **word** fixation/duration, not a line-order sequence. No cited study validates long line residence as a focus-window trigger. |
| `w12 → [gap] → w16` or a line change across a scroll/reflow | Unclassified continuity break. | Data-quality decision, not skipping or another reading behavior. Do not infer the movement across the gap. |

**Important non-sequence evidence.** [GazePrompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772) also triggered a word aid on a first fixation over 500 ms, more than four same-word refixations, or more than 1,500 ms total one-pass word fixation. Those were adjustable system thresholds, not order classes or validated NewsMead values. [von der Malsburg et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25530253/) and [Mézière et al. (2024)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2023.2232063) studied continuous scanpath-regularity measures rather than discrete order labels; their findings varied with sentence or assessment context. [Strukelj and Niehorster (2018)](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/Strukelj) reported gaze profiles under **instructed** regular reading, thorough reading, skimming, and spell checking; those task names are not automatically inferred from one path. [Schotter et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24747167/) found that functionally preventing regressions harmed comprehension in their reading experiment; therefore, reducing backward movement is not a sound success criterion by itself.
