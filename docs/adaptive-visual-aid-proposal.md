# NewsMead implementation proposal: word and line scanpaths to adaptive visual aids

**Version:** 2026-09-23. **Status:** implementation specification for development and validation; no claim that the complete feature improves reading yet. **Target task:** adults aged 45+ reading news in NewsMead. The feature adapts during ordinary reading. Aid type, target, strength, and duration may change; a known interruption and the existing four scaffold levels are not its organizing rule.

**Implementation checkpoint 2026-10-01:** [pilot revision 6](scanpath-measurement-pilot.md) puts the integrated prototype before participant evaluation. The [runtime checkpoint](scanpath-aid-runtime.md) now connects the [live P01–P13 engine](scanpath-detectors.md) to independent line guidance, reactive line-start cues, localized magnification and optional context focus, with reader controls and action/render provenance. Word and focus prominence vary with observed residence under an explicit experimental profile. An established ongoing prefix adds duration without inventing a finalized refixation. Synthetic parity covers 183 raw prefixes; focused policy/pipeline checks and the debug build pass. Device execution, matched raw evaluation instrumentation, human validity and benefit remain pending; the requested stronger provider model remains unverified.

**Aid design decision:** implement independent reading aids selected from scanpath
events, with strength parameters inside each aid type. The owner permits replacing
the legacy scaffold design. The [aid design and evidence contract](scanpath-adaptive-aid-design.md)
records the complete P01–P13-to-action relationships, primary-source locations,
limits and migration. Line guidance and local word support are the primary forms;
digital ruler/underline and word-progress coloring are explicit candidate variants
with separate form IDs. A0–A4 are identifiers, not levels or a required ladder.
No reviewed study here validates the ordinal WORD → LINE → FOCUS → REENTRY
progression.

## Research and product question

Can NewsMead derive reliable ordered **word and line** gaze-to-text events on a phone, distinguish observable reading-navigation patterns, and use those patterns to offer visual help that improves independently measured reading outcomes? The sequence is the central object. Fixation and dwell measurements help construct it and describe its timing; a count or weighted “reading instability” score is not the classifier.

A published scanpath class is a precedent for an **observable movement**, not proof that a NewsMead reader is confused. A published visual aid is a precedent for **a response in its tested population and hardware**, not proof that a NewsMead trigger is correct. Every mapping below states both links.

~~~text
MGazeNet observation + capture time + validity + current article geometry
  -> candidate fixations and explicit breaks
  -> fixation-to-word and fixation-to-line assignments, including ambiguity
  -> ordered word-fixation path + word visits + line visits
  -> descriptive transition and duration events, or unclassified
  -> evidence-gated policy {aid type, target, prominence, duration, reason}
  -> article display + bounded action audit (matched raw replay instrument pending)
~~~

## How NewsMead derives the scanpaths

1. **Record the observation.** Use the existing provider observation with capture time, delivery ID/time, validity reason, raw and displayed screen coordinates, article/text identity, layout revision, scroll state, and the rendered TextView geometry. Use the capture-time domain for intervals; delivery or wall-clock time measures latency. Never join observations across an invalid sample, a long unobserved gap, scrolling, calibration change, or text reflow.
2. **Find candidate fixations.** Group temporally contiguous, spatially compact raw gaze samples with a reproducible detector, retaining start/end time, sample count, centroid, dispersion, and censor flags. [Salvucci and Goldberg (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1145/355017.355028) support the method family, not NewsMead's parameter values. The current offline I-DT defaults (100 ms, at least three samples, 125 ms maximum gap, half a line-height dispersion) are provisional; the first natural-reading recording had a median 58.63 ms sample interval. Call these **candidate fixations** until independently checked.
3. **Map each candidate to rendered text.** Use the coordinate stream that generated the candidate and its corresponding Android layout/AOI assignments. Current offline replay uses raw coordinates and raw-derived sample AOIs; displayed smoothed coordinates are retained separately. A future displayed-stream method needs its own matched mapping and evaluation rather than mixing raw detection with smoothed assignment. Keep article UTF-16 offsets, a document-order word ordinal, physical line index, layout version, assignment support, and alternatives when ambiguous. A word can be stable in document order while its physical line changes after reflow. Do not convert a nearby-line hit into an exact-line hit, or force a word label from uncertain horizontal position.
4. **Preserve three related sequences.** The full fixation path retains repeated fixations on the same word, so a same-word refixation remains visible. Consecutive eligible fixations on one word can also form a **word visit** with observed residence time; eligible fixations/visits on one physical line form a **line visit**. Word and line paths are separate projections of the same timestamped evidence. Residence time is observed fixation time, not elapsed time across an unobserved interval.
5. **Compute transitions only within continuous eligible runs.** For two assigned word fixations, store the signed difference in document-order word ordinals and whether the line changed. For line visits, store signed physical-line displacement, article offset ranges, and whether the motion is consistent with a line-end-to-next-line-start return sweep. A backward motion in screen x alone is not a regression. Keep exact displacements and timing even when a named event is emitted.
6. **Emit a break or unclassified event when evidence is insufficient.** Gaps, noisy candidates, ambiguous word/line assignment, off-text gaze, scrolling, layout change, or missing capture time break the relevant sequence. The word path may break while a line remains assignable; a reliable line does not license an invented word path.

For example, word fixations 12 -> 13 -> 12 -> 13 show a word revisit, while line visits 4 -> 5 -> 4 -> 5 show a line revisit. They are not interchangeable. Two consecutive fixations on word 12 are a same-word refixation, not a revisit after leaving it. A jump from word 12 to word 15 says only that the intervening words were not **observed as assigned fixations**; it does not prove they were unread.

For the slow-progress example, record **observed line residence** as the sum of eligible fixation durations on that line, together with the rendered text amount and covered time. Where word assignments are reliable, also record forward document-word displacement per observed second and backward returns separately. A line-only sequence cannot locate slow movement *within* a line; it can report only residence. Compare like text lengths, layout, and each reader's measured variation before calling a duration unusually long. These quantities describe pace and exposure, not a validated need for a focus aid.

The offline analyzer and live article engine derive provisional fixation candidates, word and line visits, assignment-quality fields, signed edges, breaks and P01–P13 patterns. The article now connects these to the independent aid policy, adjustable displays, reader controls and action records. Finalized pattern evidence and established ongoing-prefix residence have separate contracts; an ongoing prefix does not increment refixations. Exact settings, display rules, bounded history and trace limits are saved in the [runtime checkpoint](scanpath-aid-runtime.md). Participant measurement follows the integrated build and matched evaluation instrument. We will evaluate **word and line viability independently**; word-level feasibility on this phone is not assumed.

## Where the order categories come from

| Published operational class or finding | What NewsMead will measure | Boundary of the evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Olivier, Guérin-Dugué, and Durand (2022)](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.15.4.5) encoded signed **words crossed between consecutive fixations** as Fwd (+1), Fwd+ (>1), Rfx (same word), Bwd (-1), and Bwd- (<-1). Their information-seeking study used an EyeLink 1000 and a duration-aware HSMM. | Preserve the fixation-level signed word change and these descriptive distinctions when both words are confidently assigned. Keep durations alongside the order series. | Their fitted states (normal reading, search, speed reading, slow confirmation) are task interpretations, not NewsMead difficulty labels or ready-made controller states. Their state duration is not a single long fixation. |
| [Yamaya, Topić, and Aizawa (2017)](https://doi.org/10.2197/ipsjjip.25.100) classified transitions between horizontally progressing **segments** as Return Sweep, Progressing, Short Re-reading, Long Re-reading, Skipping, and Resuming. | Form word and line segment transitions; separately mark line-end-to-next-line-start sweeps, same-line backward returns, and multiline returns when geometry supports them. | Their 11-reader Tobii TX300 classifier served fixation-to-word alignment. Its 87% mapping result, and weak F1 for Short Re-reading (0.47) and Skipping (0.31), do not validate a phone classifier or these as aid triggers. NewsMead will not call an observation gap “skipping.” |
| [Parker, Slattery, and Kirkby (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2018.12.007) studied multiline return sweeps and corrective movements. [Carr et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0) studied vertical-drift correction and line assignment. | Retain return-sweep versus between-line return candidates and ambiguous vertical assignments. | Both are method/behavior precedents under precise eye tracking; neither supplies phone thresholds. |
| [von der Malsburg and Vasishth (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2011.02.004) distinguished different rereading paths in sentence reanalysis. [von der Malsburg, Kliegl, and Vasishth (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12208) related scanpath regularity to reader and text factors. | Preserve the complete ordered path for later sequence summaries, rather than only transition totals. | Their path families and regularity associations are context dependent. They do not establish a momentary “struggling” state for phone news reading. |

**NewsMead's own operational labels:** word step, word jump, same-word refixation, word return, return sweep, same-line return, multiline return, repeated revisit, line residence, and break. These labels name geometry and time, never comprehension or support need. A “long residence” or “slow progression” flag is a **duration descriptor**, adjusted for text amount and observation coverage; it is not an order class. Numerical thresholds, window lengths, and persistence rules must be tuned on development material and frozen before evaluation. No supervised training or weighted score is required to run this descriptive classifier.

The [published sequence-to-behavior table](scanpath-sequence-behavior-table.md) lists every named movement class and longer path family used as a precedent here, with each study's observation, interpretation, and transfer limit. Its NewsMead examples are explicitly schematic rather than participant traces.

## Confidence and weights for adaptive decisions

The [quantitative source audit](scanpath-reading-difficulty-evidence.md#can-the-published-numbers-become-newsmead-decision-weights) found study-level correlations, coefficients, cluster frequencies, and score-prediction results, but **no calibrated probability that a current NewsMead path means difficulty or that a particular visual aid will help**. For example, [Berlin Khenis et al. (2024), Table 4](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/12/11/112/pdf#page=12) estimated how an independent comprehension score predicts paragraph-path cluster use; [Ma et al. (2023), Table 3](https://d-nb.info/1261722655/34) correlated a network statistic with a mixed reading score. Neither number is a portable path-to-aid weight. A model's confidence in an HSMM state or cluster membership also measures **pattern assignment**, not reader difficulty.

Retain three distinct decision quantities: (1) whether the phone measured the word/line path reliably, (2) whether that path predicts a prespecified, independent difficulty or support-need outcome for the current reader and text, and (3) whether an aid at a particular strength improves an independent reading outcome. Low measurement quality triggers **abstention**, never a stronger cue. If a weighted policy is pursued, start with a small prespecified set of observable path features and a simple regularized model; estimate its coefficients from target-population NewsMead data with aids off, rather than assigning weights from paper effect sizes. Evaluate probability calibration, precision, sensitivity, false alerts, and abstentions on untouched readers and articles. Passage-level question accuracy cannot by itself label the exact moment a cue was needed; momentary reported need and passage comprehension must remain different targets.

Finally, compare candidate aid types and strengths by randomized, matched-exposure tests. A high probability of difficulty alone does not show that a focus window, highlight, or magnifier helps. Freeze any resulting decision boundaries before an independent evaluation, and keep unvalidated weighted decisions in shadow mode. The initial descriptive scanpath classifier remains useful without a weighted difficulty score.

## From a measured event to a visual aid

The policy may return no aid. It receives event type, word/line placement quality, text anchor, layout revision, recent cue history, reader preferences, and current aid exposure. It returns an action with a reason that can be replayed. Strength can vary through contrast/prominence, supported span, and display duration; these are separately testable parameters, not an assumed four-step ladder.

Select aid type for the observed task, separately from strength within that aid.
Each rule retains its study reference and exact supported relationship; unsupported
trigger or strength extensions remain visible in its experimental status. Ordinary
forward movement updates location rather than proving difficulty. Revisit/cycle
observations do not force an aid. A localized magnifier's within-pass inspection
precedent does not validate a trigger on every earlier-word revisit.

| Observed situation | Candidate NewsMead action | Intervention evidence and precise claim | Decision before automatic use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reliable line following, line end, or return-sweep candidate | Test a legible current-line highlight or start marker while following; show the destination-line cue as the transition begins if timing permits. Compare subtle and prominent versions. | [GazePrompt (Wang et al., 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772) tested gaze-aware line highlight/arrow in 13 low-vision adults reading aloud on a 120 Hz desktop tracker. Line-switching time fell (p = .014); switching accuracy did not significantly improve. | Validate the line and **cue latency**. A cue rendered only after the reader has already found the next line may be too late to help. Compare cue timing, form, and no cue in phone reading. |
| Confident word-level repeated inspection or long word residence | Test a localized word magnifier at the confirmed word, leaving surrounding text visible; strength can vary by magnification and duration. | [GazePrompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772) triggered word magnification or speech on prolonged first fixation, refixations, or total one-pass duration. In its reading-aloud study the maximum first-pass word fixation time and misread-word count did **not** improve significantly; users often increased trigger thresholds to avoid false positives. | First establish exact-word placement and meaningful word-event coverage. Then compare magnifier with no cue on independently measured recognition/helpfulness; a long fixation alone is not proof of difficulty. |
| Reliably observed slow movement across one line or sustained line residence | **Experimental focus window:** emphasize a small current text region with adjustable width/contrast while keeping adjacent words and other lines legible. Compare with a simpler line anchor and no cue. | No located primary intervention trial establishes “slow line -> focus window helps.” [Rayner, Slattery, and Bélanger (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3075059/) found reading rate depended on the available moving-window span, differently for fast and slow young adults. [Paterson et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23066801/) used a gaze-contingent filtered window in older adults. These are **perceptual-span measurements**, not focus-aid benefits. | Determine whether the slow event is real rather than poor tracking, text complexity, or deliberate reading. Test whether the window helps place keeping or comprehension and whether dimming removes useful preview. The current five-line FOCUS renderer is an asset to modify, not a validated response. |
| Repeated same-line or earlier-line returns | In development, compare no cue, a stable line/region anchor, and a focus-region variant; never block rereading. | [Schotter, Tran, and Rayner (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614531148) found comprehension suffered when readers could not reread previously seen text. GazePrompt supported line jumps, but did not isolate benefit for repeated revisits. | Establish that the return was correctly measured and that the chosen cue helps rather than interrupts useful rereading. A stronger cue is a test condition, not an escalation rule inherited from a study. |
| Candidate placekeeping form for reliably anchored line reading or reader-requested help | Add a line bar/underline as an alternative to the existing focus display. Slow residence/returns can supply experimental observations, not an established automatic trigger. | [Niklaus et al. (2023), Digital Reading Rulers](https://thereadabilityconsortium.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Digital-Reading-Rulers-Evaluating-Inclusively-Designed-Rulers-for-Readers-With-Dyslexia-and-Without.pdf#page=11) tested mouse-controlled rulers. Grey Bar improved speed versus no ruler in the screened/self-report dyslexia group; a slow-scanpath trigger was not tested. | Test form, trigger and strength separately after the integrated build. Preserve context and reader control. Detailed populations, null outcomes and corrected pairwise comparisons are in the [aid design contract](scanpath-adaptive-aid-design.md). |
| Ambiguous assignment, missing samples, scroll/reflow | Clear or hold only a safe static cue; suppress new gaze-triggered actions until reacquisition. | [Carr et al.](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0) show that line attribution can fail under drift; this row is a safety design choice. | Measure how often abstention prevents wrong cues and how often it withholds potentially helpful support. |

The [scanpath-to-visual-aid evidence map](scanpath-visual-aid-evidence-map.md) expands these rows with line-start bolding, fixation-linked word coloring, paragraph progress cues, known-interruption bookmarks, and the measured limits of magnification and focus-region aids. It identifies which studies measured a matching movement and which tested an aid without validating the proposed NewsMead trigger.

The focus-window example is retained as a **research candidate**, with an explicit possible downside: gaze-contingent moving-window experiments demonstrate that readers use text outside the fixation point. Restricting it can slow reading, and “slow” readers are not a single support-needs group. The proposed window therefore starts as a lightly emphasized, context-preserving variant; dark peripheral masking is a separately evaluated condition, not the default. A reader may prefer no cue. [Cheong et al. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19661837/) also found that a physical line guide improved some vertical-control behavior in people with age-related macular degeneration while increasing corrective movements and slightly reducing speed. A cue can help one outcome and hurt another.

**Aid combinations** are allowed only as separately logged/tested actions. GazePrompt users sometimes preferred combined line and word support, but that preference does not establish additive benefit on NewsMead. The policy should never infer “difficulty” solely from dwell or force a cue on ordinary rereading.

## Build, evaluation and release sequence

1. **Complete the integrated prototype.** Reuse the offline P01–P13 reference and existing gaze evidence. Build the Android causal word/line engine, pattern-to-aid policy, variable-strength A0–A4 renderers, controls and action/render records. Exercise the full path with deterministic fixtures and causal replay, including uncertainty, stale geometry, release and user-off. Complete the Android build and then its matched evaluation instrument. No new participant measurement is a prerequisite for implementing this prototype; candidate trigger/strength rules remain explicitly experimental.
2. **Matched task evaluation after the build checkpoint.** Use separately authorized, timestamped raw placement/continuous-order tasks under documented phone/font/posture/dot conditions. Report conditional intended-target/order agreement, confusion, missing/extra events and extraction coverage. The single smoothed protocol-v6 run (47.5% exact-word; 61.6% guided exact-line) is baseline context, not a raw or natural-order score. Annotation of the same estimated stream can audit processing/scroll/uncertainty, but supplies no independent eye-position reference. The sparse first natural diagnostic remains diagnostic evidence.
3. **Classifier feasibility and support-need association.** Freeze definitions on development readers/passages, evaluate on untouched task references, and report per-class precision/recall only where those references label the movement. Report ordinary-reading extraction/coverage without natural eye-truth accuracy claims. Test features against independent help reports and passage outcomes; prespecify pre-report windows/lead time and exclude report-control interaction/recovery from prediction inputs. A passage score cannot label momentary difficulty. Word-level failure may permit a separately evaluated line/region aid, retaining shared detector limits. Check return-sweep geometry, sensitivity and layout boundaries.
4. **Aid validation.** Run controlled experimental displays after relevant placement/software checks; a known-target or reported-help task can evaluate an aid before an automatic difficulty trigger is validated. Randomize/counterbalance the conditions suited to the selected question, including a no-cue baseline. Test line placement/timing, local word magnification and context-preserving focus variants with independent task responses, comprehension, unwanted/wrong cues, distraction and preference. A line anchor can be the comparator for a focus-region question; a universal three-arm design is not required. Fewer returns/triggers or lower dwell alone do not establish benefit.
5. **Adaptive strength test.** Compare strength parameters within one aid type/context, then freeze an evidence-supported varying-strength rule. Compare it with constant strength using the same type/event/timing eligibility logic, planned comparable exposure opportunities and actual exposure reporting. Use a static comparator when testing timing/location adaptation. Keep evaluation readers/passages untouched; a nonsignificant small-sample comprehension result cannot establish no harm.
6. **Deployment boundary.** Keep unvalidated mappings in shadow or manually inspected study mode. Enable each automatic action only after its placement and benefit gates pass. Preserve no-aid and user-off paths. Coordinate, timestamp, and concurrency behavior need focused software review and replay checks; software tests alone do not establish human validity.

For cue timing, retain first supporting and last decisive capture times, evidence
availability, decision and render/frame acknowledgment in a documented common
monotonic clock. Measure total capture-to-render intervals as well as component
delays; identify any visibility proxy. Compare prospective/reactive cues against
prompted transitions and independent responses, since a return-sweep cue can arrive
after localization. These measurements do not establish physiological eye-to-aid
latency.

**Implementation decision:** build both word and line scanpath derivation, classify published-style observable transitions at the level that is actually measurable, and evaluate line cue, word magnifier, and focus-region candidates against independent reading outcomes. Word level is a planned feasibility track, not an excluded feature. Slow line progression is an observation that may justify testing a focus window, not evidence that the window already helps. The [primary-paper ledger](scanpath-scaffolding-evidence.md) records the source populations, devices, findings, and transfer limits.
