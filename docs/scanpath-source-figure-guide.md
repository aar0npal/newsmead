# Original scanpath figures: where to look and what they establish

**Checked 2026-09-25.** This is a reading guide to original source pages and images. It contains no reconstructed scanpath graphics. It focuses on *longer gaze paths and task strategies*, rather than one-fixation movement codes. A gaze plot with successive fixations can show order; a heatmap alone cannot.

**For the separate question of whether a path indicates reading difficulty, use the [primary-study difficulty evidence guide](scanpath-reading-difficulty-evidence.md).** Most named paths below were descriptive and were not tested against an independent difficulty or comprehension outcome.

## 1. “Layer Cake” — scanning a structured web page

- **Original article:** Kara Pernice, [*The Layer-Cake Pattern of Scanning Content on the Web*](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) (Nielsen Norman Group, 2019). This is a practitioner research article, not a peer-reviewed journal article; it has no DOI.
- **Look at the source's [TechCrunch article gaze plot](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2019/07/19/p13_commit_layercake_cropped_techcrunch-com_watermrkd.png).** The participant visits prominent subheadings across the page, enters body text beneath a relevant heading, then continues to later subheadings. The article describes this image immediately after its definition.
- **Look at the original-study [dog-training gaze plot](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2019/07/19/dogbreedz_layercake_watermrkd.png).** The same article says this came from its 2004 study. The [NN/g research report](https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/styleguide/resources/How_People_Read_on_the_Web.pdf) places the example on **printed p. 81**, with context on printed pp. 79–82. The PDF viewer page index could not be independently verified because the large report did not load in the available viewer.
- **Observed path:** repeated heading/subheading visits, with selective entries into body text. Its spatial unit is **page sections**, not successive physical text lines or word indices.
- **What was checked:** visual observation of gaze plots in a task where the participant browsed web content. The accessible article does not provide a per-pattern classifier test, comprehension outcome, or sufficient hardware/participant details for the pictured individual. It is direct evidence that this *page-scanning shape occurs*, not a validated marker of difficulty or aid need.

The article also shows a [nine-person heatmap](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) as group evidence for attention to subheadings, but that heatmap does **not** preserve each reader's scanpath order. Read the individual gaze plots for order.

## 2. Other named NN/g page-scanning patterns

The same applied research program groups four web text patterns in [*Text Scanning Patterns: Eyetracking Evidence*](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/) (Pernice, 2019). The individual gaze plots below preserve fixation order; the article's aggregate heatmaps do not. These are **descriptive web-task labels**, not separately validated mental states or four independent studies.

The fourth source label is **Layer Cake**, linked in §1. NN/g's terms are useful descriptions of webpage scan shapes and reading coverage. Their article does not give enough per-image method detail to transplant these as validated NewsMead behavior classes.

## 3. “Cue-led search and verification” — question → passage → question

- **Original journal article:** Faisal Al Saidi and Tayba Al Hilali (2026), [*Eye-tracking study of L2 learners’ reading paths: new insights into reading strategies*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6), DOI [10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6). The abstract uses the phrase **“cue-led search and verification”**; the authors name the proposed process **“anchor-search-confirm.”** The article is online with figure numbers rather than stable printed journal pages.
- **Start with the source's [Fig. 10: “Keywords as comprehension anchors”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6/figures/10).** This is the participant example the Results section uses to show attention to a question cue, search for a matching passage cue (including the number “17”), reading near that cue, and returns between passage and question.
- **Then open [Fig. 11: “Reading Path”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6/figures/11) and [Fig. 12: “Anchor-search-confirm reading”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6/figures/12).** These are the **authors’ explanatory process figures**, not independent participant traces. Earlier [Fig. 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6/figures/1) shows recorded reading-path snapshots and [Fig. 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00408-6/figures/2) combines them.
- **Observed path:** a salient word or number in a visible question → search for the matching or related location in the passage → repeated cross-checking between the two displayed regions. The units are **question and passage areas, with salient cue words**, not a general line-order string.
- **What was checked:** 24 Omani Arabic-L1 college L2 readers, ages 19–20, on a 60 Hz Tobii Pro Nano; gaze episodes plus stimulated-recall interviews. Eighteen of 24 began the detail-question phase at the question panel. Two coders agreed on 58/60 double-coded gaze episodes (96.7%). The article's visit-derived question/passage switching proxy rose from 8.0 to 12.4 per minute from main-idea to detail questions. The authors say they lacked a text-only baseline, answer-accuracy outcomes, complete fixation-sequence exports for the full dataset, and complete calibration/track-loss summaries. They call the broader interpretation a **working hypothesis**, not a validated classifier or proof of shallow comprehension.

This is a close precedent only when NewsMead presents an **external question or goal cue** beside a passage. A reader simply looking back within an article is not thereby following this named strategy.

## 4. Empirical whole-path rereading families in prose

- **Original journal article:** von der Malsburg and Vasishth (2011), [*What is the scanpath signature of syntactic reanalysis?*](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2011.pdf), DOI [10.1016/j.jml.2011.02.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2011.02.004).
- **Go directly to Fig. 6, journal p. 120**: empirical prototypes of three sentence-region path clusters after a regression from region 9. **A** returns to the sentence beginning and rereads all or part; **B** makes one regression to early material, followed by trial end; **C** returns locally toward disambiguating region 8. **Fig. 9, journal p. 121** shows the finer exploratory 14-cluster solution.
- **External check:** in the Spanish garden-path experiment, only **A** was significantly more frequent for garden-path than comparison sentences. B and C were not. This is a condition association for one path family, not proof that every A trace is a comprehension failure or that an aid helps it. The figure's units are **sentence regions**, not NewsMead word or physical-line labels.

## 5. Four expository-text reading profiles with summary performance

- **Original journal paper:** Hyönä, Lorch, and Kaakinen (2002), [*Individual differences in reading to summarize expository text: Evidence from eye fixation patterns*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.94.1.44), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 94:44–55. An [author-uploaded full-text preview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228918114_Individual_differences_in_reading_to_summarize_expository_text_Evidence_from_eye_fixation_patterns) is available; its accessibility may depend on ResearchGate.
- **Read Figs. 2–4, journal pp. 48–50**, for the measured reinspection and look-back profiles across sentence positions; **Table 8, p. 53**, for summary accuracy. These are **aggregate fixation-profile plots, not raw numbered scanpath snapshots**.
- **Named profiles:** *fast linear* readers rarely reinspected; *slow linear* readers reinspected sentences before moving on; *nonselective reviewers* looked back to earlier sentences; *topic-structure processors* attended to headings and preferentially looked back from section endings. Forty-seven analyzed university readers read two expository texts for a summary task.
- **External checks:** profile membership was reasonably stable between texts (chance-corrected κ = .77), and topic-structure processors produced more accurate summaries than slow linear readers. This is a reader-level association under a summary task, not a validated moment-by-moment detector; one cluster contained only three readers.

## 6. Fluent, intermediate, and beginner sentence-path prototypes

- **Original journal paper:** Parshina et al. (2022), [*Monolingual and bilingual reading processes in Russian: An exploratory scanpath analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.414), with [author manuscript PDF](https://tmalsburg.github.io/ParshinaEtAl2022.pdf).
- **Go to [Fig. 4, near PDF viewer p. 60](https://tmalsburg.github.io/ParshinaEtAl2022.pdf#page=60)** for scanpaths colored by cluster, then **[Fig. 5, near PDF viewer p. 61](https://tmalsburg.github.io/ParshinaEtAl2022.pdf#page=61)** for a real scanpath nearest each cluster center. The author-manuscript text explicitly distinguishes Fig. 4's sample paths from Fig. 5's representative paths; printed manuscript pp. 23–24 describe the selection, while figures are appended near the end.
- **Observed profiles:** *fluent* shows relatively straight forward reading, some unfixated words, and few regressions; *intermediate* has slower reading and more local word returns; *beginner* has frequent word and whole-sentence rereading. The researchers studied 120 readers from Russian monolingual, child, heritage, and L2 groups on 30 short sentences.
- **External checks:** profile use differed across reader groups and related to some proficiency/comprehension measures. The authors explicitly call the clusters **descriptive summaries of a continuum**. Only correctly answered trials entered the scanpath analysis; the figure alone cannot diagnose a person's capability or need for aid.

## 7. Task-reading strategy transitions: comprehensive exploration and iterative content evaluation

- **Original journal paper:** Chen, Zhang, and Lu (2024), [*How readers attentive and inattentive to task-related information scan: An eye-tracking study*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2543925124000093), DOI [10.1016/j.dim.2024.100073](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dim.2024.100073).
- **Read Fig. 4, “Local strategy transitions,”** and its accompanying results section. Fig. 4 summarizes the authors' coded beginning/middle/end strategy transitions; it is **not a raw participant gaze plot**. Their Table I defines the gaze-area patterns behind the local strategy codes.
- **Named patterns:** *comprehensive exploration* repeatedly locates task-relevant information, evaluates it against the question, and synthesizes it; *iterative content evaluation* repeatedly compares text and question without the same breadth of navigation. Twenty-four participants performed fact-finding and content-understanding tasks in an interface with question, navigation, and text areas.
- **Evidence limit:** the “attentive/inattentive” grouping was itself defined by fixation duration on task-related paragraphs, so the subsequent gaze-strategy contrast is not an independent reading-capability ground truth. This is useful for task-specific sequence coding, but neither label is a universal prose-reading state.

## How to use these figures in the proposal

The strongest visual evidence for **order** here is an individual numbered gaze plot, a recorded gaze episode, or a cluster prototype that is itself a real participant path. Heatmaps, aggregate reinspection plots, and authors' explanatory diagrams answer different questions. The source studies document page-section scanning, question-led cross-checking, sentence rereading, and summary-task profiles under different tasks and layouts. None publishes `L4 → L5 → L4 → L5` as a named or validated cognitive class. If NewsMead uses any of these names, it must first reproduce the necessary context and establish that its phone gaze measurements can distinguish the path from mapping error and normal reading. Aid benefit requires its own outcome study.
