# Scanpath patterns and reading difficulty: primary-study evidence

**Checked 2026-09-25.** This is a source guide for the NewsMead question: which *ordered gaze paths* have been linked to an independent measure of reading difficulty, reading ability, comprehension, or controlled processing difficulty? It contains no reconstructed figures. Open the original figures in the linked papers to inspect the paths.

## Finding

No reviewed paper establishes a single scanpath shape as a conclusive, real-time sign that an older person is struggling with a news article on a phone. Several studies find **group-level associations** between ordered paths and independently assessed reading skill or comprehension. Other studies show that rereading can accompany a difficult sentence, a deliberate reading goal, or *better* comprehension. These results justify testing candidate patterns in NewsMead; they do not validate a fixed path-to-aid rule.

The words *difficulty* and *ability* are used precisely below. A diagnosis or standardized comprehension score is a **reader-level** outcome. A hard or ambiguous sentence is a **text-condition** manipulation. Answer accuracy after a passage is a **comprehension** outcome. None is interchangeable with moment-by-moment aid need.

## Why a universal rereading rule fails

| Original source and figure | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Markevich & Streltsova (2025), [journal article](https://doi.org/10.3390/jemr18060060), [Fig. 6 individual paths and Figs. 7–8 genre/comprehension results](https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/18/6/60) | Among 44 adolescents, forward and regressive path clusters changed with **genre**. Higher comprehension was associated with more regressive patterns for expository and poetic text, but more forward patterns for narrative text. This directly contradicts the general rule that regressive paths imply poor comprehension. It remains an exploratory association, not an aid trial. |
| von der Malsburg & Vasishth (2011), [original PDF, Fig. 6, journal p. 120](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2011.pdf#page=12) | One path family reread much of a sentence after encountering a Spanish garden-path ambiguity. It was more frequent in ambiguous than comparison sentences (136 vs. 101 instances); other regression-path families were not condition-associated. Comprehension accuracy averaged 92%, so the path reflects a processing challenge in that experiment, **not demonstrated failure**. The authors did not claim a conclusive signature. |
| Christianson et al. (2024), [publisher article, Fig. 7](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749596X24000184) | Data-derived rereading path clusters occurred after temporarily ambiguous English sentences. Ambiguity increased extensive rereading, but patterns of rereading did not consistently predict successful repair of the misinterpretation across two experiments. |
| Olkoniemi et al. (2024), [original PDF, Fig. 2 and Tables 4–5](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/0163853X.2023.2266964) | Fast linear, selective rereading, and nonselective rereading path clusters were clearly illustrated. The study explicitly tested their relationship with memory and inference-question accuracy and found **no significant comprehension-accuracy association**. A clear path taxonomy is therefore not automatically a difficulty taxonomy. |
| Mézière et al. (2025), [original read-only study](https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.70023), [author-institution PDF](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/186844/Reading%20Research%20Quarterly%20-%202025%20-%20M%C3%A9zi%C3%A8re%20-%20Using%20Eye%20Movements%20From%20a%20Read%20Only%20Task%20to%20Predict%20Text%20Comprehension.pdf?sequence=1) | In 62 adults reading fictional stories, slower reading and longer total reading time predicted **better** recall, while some more efficient eye measures also predicted better recall. This paper did not classify whole paths; it is a check against interpreting slow progression alone as difficulty. |

## News/article reading check

A bounded search found no primary **news-reading** study that simultaneously provides (1) inspectable multi-fixation article paths and (2) a tested association between those paths and an independent difficulty or comprehension outcome. The nearest studies answer related but narrower questions:

| Primary news study | What was measured | Why it does not validate a difficulty path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Zambarbieri et al. (2008), *Eye Tracking Analysis in Reading Online Newspapers*](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2279), [original PDF](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/download/2279/3475/8503) | Gaze while browsing two Italian newspaper home pages and reading article pages; a questionnaire assessed some recall. | The paper studies page navigation, coverage and usability. It does not show that a particular ordered article path predicted an individual's questionnaire result or difficulty. |
| [Kruikemeier et al. (2018), *Learning From News on Different Media Platforms*](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2017.1388310) | Eye-tracked visual attention and later knowledge for 149 people reading print or website news. | Attention to different stories related to broad versus topic-specific learning. The analysis is about **which stories received attention and for how long**, not a word/line scanpath within an article that signals difficulty. |
| [Greussing et al. (2020), *Learning From Science News via Interactive and Animated Data Visualizations*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1075547020962100) | In 45 readers, supplemental Figs. 2a–c show AOI visit sequences across first text, graphic and second text. Memory was independently tested. | Longer fixation on the second text part mediated a learning benefit for one interactive-animated condition. The three-area *order* was described, but no named path class was tested as a difficulty or failed-comprehension sign. |
| [Ohme et al. (2022), *Mobile News Learning*](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2021.2000082), [institutional copy](https://www.weizenbaum-library.de/items/ae8f92f5-993a-417d-b78a-912b8eabc39a) | Phone-versus-desktop social newsfeed exposure, gaze attention, and political knowledge in 122 students. The phone condition used a **30 Hz Tobii tracker placed below the phone**. | This is hardware- and content-adjacent evidence, but it measured post attention and learning rather than ordered within-article word/line patterns. Smartphone knowledge gain was lower and was **not explained by measured visual attention**. |

Studies that use news to predict *relevance*, *credibility*, or *believability* answer different questions. Berlin Khenis et al. included newspaper-like material in the **separate comprehension assessment**, while the eye-tracked material was expository paragraphs; it is not a news-article scanpath validation.

## Audit of five suggested “difficulty patterns”

The following labels were supplied for source checking. They must not be cited as an established five-class taxonomy.

| Suggested label | Source check | Verdict and defensible replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **“Tangled/Clustered” = lexical decoding failure or dyslexia** | [Ma et al. (2023), Fig. 5, PDF p. 12](https://d-nb.info/1261722655/34#page=12) compared averaged **word-transition networks** in adults reading science text. Its *transitivity* is a graph clustering statistic. The study did not test dyslexia, phonological decoding, within-word movements, or the claimed 4–6-character loops. The separately linked [Mézière et al. (2024), Fig. 1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2023.2232063#page=7) illustrates regular versus irregular paths, not a clinical decoding sign. | The name and diagnosis claim are unsupported. Say only that repeated word transitions/network structure were **associated** with a comprehension/reading composite in Ma et al.; [Parshina et al. (2022), Figs. 4–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8853123/) is the more directly relevant *high-risk-of-dyslexia* sentence-path study, still not a diagnostic trigger. |
| **“Ping-Pong” = syntactic misunderstanding and reparsing** | In the exact linked [von der Malsburg & Vasishth paper, *Scanpaths reveal syntactic underspecification and reanalysis strategies*](https://www.ling.uni-potsdam.de/~vasishth/pdfs/MalsburgVasishthLCP2013.pdf), the authors' path groups are **rereading, checking, and rapid regressions**; see Fig. 3, journal p. 19 / PDF p. 20. They manipulated Spanish sentence attachment and measured comprehension, but did not define a “Ping-Pong” class. | The paper supports studying how some regression paths vary with syntactic ambiguity. It does not mean every repeated back-and-forth path is a misunderstood sentence or that rereading necessarily repaired comprehension; see the later [Christianson et al. (2024) test](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2024.104515). |
| **“Staircase” = line-targeting deficit and comprehension loss** | [Mézière et al. (2024), Fig. 1, journal p. 85](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2023.2232063#page=7) shows examples of sentence/passage path regularity. It does not define line-targeting or test a “Staircase” class. In a direct return-sweep study, [Slattery & Vasilev (2019), Fig. 2 and Tables 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6647890/), **57%** of ordinary adults' line-initial fixations were followed by a corrective leftward saccade. | The name and difficulty interpretation are unsupported. Return-sweep undershoots/corrections are real, common oculomotor events; the study's line-start bolding reduced corrections without improving page reading time. They cannot alone establish losing one's place or failed comprehension. |
| **“Nonselective rereading” = mind wandering, ADHD, zero comprehension** | [Olkoniemi et al. (2024), Fig. 2, journal p. 15](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/0163853X.2023.2266964) genuinely identified a **nonselective rereading** cluster. It did **not** measure ADHD or mind wandering; memory/inference-question accuracy had no significant association with cluster membership. A separate [meta-analysis of mind wandering during reading](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01797-8) instead found more skipping and fewer fixations on average during probe-identified mind wandering. | The path label is real; the attached psychological and clinical claims are unsupported. Preserve it only as the observed extensive-rereading path. |
| **“Random Jump-Ahead” = blind guessing in prose** | The cited [Tablatin & Rodrigo study](https://archium.ateneo.edu/discs-faculty-pubs/46/) is about **students debugging Java code**; its Fig. 1 compares common paths of higher- and lower-performing groups. The authors describe jumps among statements that do not follow program logic, but do not name a “Random Jump-Ahead” class or measure guessing. | Task and mechanism were changed in the suggested account. It cannot be transferred to news prose or interpreted as “blind guessing” without a new study. |

The Facebook post, YouTube video and unrelated figure links in the suggested list are not primary evidence for these five mechanisms. The directly linked papers above provide the inspectable figures and tested outcomes.

## Can the published numbers become NewsMead decision weights?

**No published number reviewed here is a calibrated probability that an individual reader needs an aid after a particular observed path.** Several papers report uncertainty about a *study-level association*, a coefficient, or the frequency of a cluster. Those answer different questions from `P(current difficulty | current path)` and from whether showing a stronger aid would help.

| Study and exact result | What the number means | Why it cannot be copied as an aid weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Berlin Khenis et al. (2024), Table 4, PDF p. 12](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/12/11/112/pdf#page=12): comprehension-score coefficient **+0.98** (SE **0.29**, *p* = .0001) for use of the faster paragraph-path cluster. | In the study's fitted model, an independently measured reader score predicted **cluster use** among 28 adolescents. | This is the opposite conditional direction from predicting current difficulty *from* a path. No held-out reader prediction, calibration, or aid outcome was reported. The coefficient is in that model's units, not a portable 0.98 weight. |
| [Mézière et al. (2024), Table 2, journal p. 89](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10888438.2023.2232063#page=10): scanpath density as a single YARC-score predictor had **R² = .07**; its coefficient was **3.6** (95% credible interval **0.6 to 6.6**). | Internal model comparison for a standardized comprehension **score** in 79 young adults; the strongest predictors differed across three assessment tasks. | R² = .07 means about 7% of variation in that score was explained by that single predictor in the reported analysis; it is **not** a 7% chance of difficulty. The interval is uncertainty about a coefficient, not confidence in a live alert. |
| [Ma et al. (2023), Table 3](https://d-nb.info/1261722655/34): word-transition-network transitivity **r = −.505** with a reading/comprehension composite. | A participant-level **correlation** in 52 adults; the composite also included reading time. | Correlation magnitude is not an event probability or a usable policy coefficient. The network also discards the exact visit order and was not tested as a held-out classifier. |
| [Parshina et al. (2022), Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8853123/) and [von der Malsburg & Vasishth (2013), Fig. 3 and Appendix Table A2](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2013.pdf): logistic effects for **group/grade or text condition/working memory predicting path membership**. | These quantify how experimental conditions or reader groups relate to chosen path categories. | They do not estimate `P(difficulty | path)` on a new adult reader, let alone the effect of a NewsMead aid. Reversing the conditional requires the relevant base rates and a separately tested prediction model. |
| [Olkoniemi et al. (2024), Fig. 2 and Tables 4–5](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2023.2266964): approximately **61% fast-linear, 29% selective rereading, 10% nonselective rereading** in the analyzed paths. | These are observed **cluster shares** in the study's Finnish story-reading task. Comprehension accuracy had no significant association with membership. | A cluster's frequency is not the probability of difficulty within it. [Markevich & Streltsova (2025), Figs. 6–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12641876/) further show that the relation between regressive paths and comprehension changes with genre. |

Predictive models are closer to the desired kind of output but still answer narrower questions. [EyeBench's OneStop results](https://eyebench.github.io/eyebench/results/onestop_rc/) report an unseen-reader-and-text AUROC of **0.572** for RoBERTEye-W versus **0.550** for a text-only model when predicting a *later question's correctness* from laboratory news-passage gaze **and question text**. AUROC measures ranking across cases, not whether a particular 0.7 output means 70% risk. The [benchmark paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a3080b8e00bed560c433100a33ca6b2d-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) reports no task/model ready for real-world use. It does not test a partial path, an older phone reader, or which aid helps.

For NewsMead, keep three quantities separate: **measurement reliability** (did the phone locate the ordered word/line visits?), **outcome prediction** (does a validated path predict an independently reported difficulty or passage outcome?), and **aid benefit** (does an action at this strength improve that outcome relative to a comparable alternative?). A state probability from an HSMM, a cluster-assignment confidence, *p* value, correlation, R², odds ratio, or AUROC cannot substitute for the latter two quantities.

Published effects can justify *predeclaring candidate features* and comparing simple models. Any policy weights must be estimated from NewsMead's target reading task after word/line measurement passes, using independent labels; tuned and evaluated on separate readers and articles; checked for calibration, false alerts, and abstentions; and finally tested in randomized aid comparisons. If there is no reliable outcome model or benefit evidence, keep weighted policies in shadow mode and retain reader-controlled support.

## What the evidence permits us to say

1. **Defensible candidate observations:** extended whole-passage rereading, unusually low path regularity relative to comparable text/task, and a persistent slow/revisiting paragraph path. These are observable descriptions with varying evidence of association to reader ability or text condition; none proves a reader is struggling on a given page.
2. **Do not call single regressions, line cycles, slow progression, or any named cluster a conclusive difficulty sign.** Genre, purpose, careful reading, and measurement error can produce them; some revisiting accompanies higher comprehension.
3. **NewsMead validation target:** after confirming the phone can recover ordered word/line visits, test candidate path features against independent passage-specific comprehension and reader-reported difficulty, separating participants and articles between tuning and evaluation. Report sensitivity, false alerts, calibration, and abstentions. Test each proposed visual aid against reading outcomes rather than whether it reduces the very gaze pattern that triggered it.

This table records evidence for *pattern interpretation*. It does not claim that any focus window, highlight, or magnifier has been shown to help a person selected by one of these scanpath patterns.

**Appraisal method:** Statistical interpretation was checked using the installed `statistical-analysis` procedural skill ([Kassis et al., 2026, *Scientific Agent Skills*](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.00065)). That methods resource is not empirical evidence that any scanpath detects difficulty.
