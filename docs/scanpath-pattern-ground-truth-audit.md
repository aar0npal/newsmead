# Scanpath pattern provenance and ground-truth audit

**Checked:** 2026-09-25. **Purpose:** identify which published labels are directly defined and checked, and which NewsMead arrows are only illustrations. This accompanies the [sequence-to-behavior table](scanpath-sequence-behavior-table.md); it does not establish a validated phone detector or aid trigger.

The [original figure reading guide](scanpath-source-figure-guide.md) links to source gaze plots and figure pages for longer path patterns, including Layer Cake, anchor-search-confirm, and sentence-region rereading.

## Three different reference standards

1. **Where gaze landed:** a fixation is aligned to a known word or line, sometimes checked by human correction. This supports a *location* or displacement claim, subject to tracker and annotation error.
2. **What movement occurred:** a human annotator or explicit geometry rule labels a return sweep, same-line return, and so on. This supports a *movement* label. It does not reveal the reader's intention.
3. **What it meant:** an independently assigned task, manipulated sentence, comprehension response, reader assessment, or intervention outcome tests a *behavioral interpretation*. These anchors differ in strength. A class discovered from the same gaze path is not independent ground truth for confusion, difficulty, or aid need.

## Primary studies, with the actual reference behind each claim

| Study | Directly observed or defined | Independent anchor and concrete finding | What NewsMead may claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Yamaya, Topić & Aizawa (2017)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/25/0/25_100/_pdf), §§3–4, Fig. 2, Tables 1 and 3 | Transitions **between horizontal reading segments** were manually assigned one of six labels: Return Sweep, Progressing, Short Re-reading, Long Re-reading, Skipping, Resuming. One annotator labeled **1,606** transitions from 11 readers and 33 document trials on a Tobii TX300. | Human labels were the reference for the random-forest classifier: F1 **0.91** for Return Sweep, **0.47** for Short Re-reading, **0.31** for Skipping, overall **0.78**. In a separate fixation-to-word evaluation, four human annotators supplied the reference mapping; the classifier-assisted pipeline reported **87.0%** exact mapping. The transition classifier used two *future* transitions, so the result is not live causal performance. | Strongest directly checked **segment-movement taxonomy** in this set. Its unit, hardware, and annotation protocol differ from NewsMead physical-line visits; low F1 for some labels requires separate local validation. A label such as “Skipping” describes an observed trajectory, not proof that text was unprocessed. |
| [Olivier, Guérin-Dugué & Durand (2022)](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/download/8415/11898/39368), pp. 4–6, 8–11 | Consecutive **word-assigned fixations** are encoded by signed word distance: `Fwd+` (>1), `Fwd` (+1), `Rfx` (same word), `Bwd` (−1), `Bwd−` (<−1). Fifteen retained adults read topic-relevance excerpts with a 1,000 Hz EyeLink. | Word positions make the five movement codes operationally checkable. The hidden semi-Markov model fitted runs of these codes; “normal reading,” “information search,” “fast reading,” and “slow confirmation” were interpretations of **latent states**, supported by additional timing/text covariates. There was no independently labeled phase sequence or held-out phase-classification test. | Cite the five **word displacement codes**, conditional on accurate word assignment. Do not present a one-arrow phase label or the fitted phases as ground-truth difficulty classes. |
| [Parker, Slattery & Kirkby (2019)](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/31639/1/VR_Rev2_TJS_JAK.pdf), methods/results and Figs. 2–5 | In multiline reading, a return sweep moves from near the end of one line toward the next line's start. An **undersweep** lands short and may be followed by a leftward corrective saccade on the new line. | Forty-seven adults and 48 children read known multiline passages with a 1,000 Hz EyeLink. The text layout anchors the intended line change; measured landing and correction trajectories support the oculomotor distinction. | `L_i(end) → L_(i+1)(start)` is a defensible schematic return sweep. A leftward movement after landing can be a normal correction, so screen-left motion alone is not rereading. No difficulty or aid-need label was tested. |
| [Carr et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0), methods/results, Figs. 3 and 7 | Compared vertical-drift correction and line-assignment algorithms, including simulated within-line and between-line regressions. | Natural-line assignment was checked against **consensus manual corrections** on a 48-trial subset, alongside simulations with known lines. This is a reference for *which line* a fixation belongs to, with its own annotation limits. | Use to justify verifying line assignment and guarding against false line returns. It does not establish a taxonomy of cognitive reading behaviors or the meaning of `L5 → L4 → L5`. |
| [von der Malsburg & Vasishth (2011)](https://tmalsburg.github.io/MalsburgVasishth2011.pdf), pp. 10–15 | Similarity clustering of **whole sentence-region fixation paths** after a regression produced A (return to sentence beginning and reread), B (brief return to early material), and C (nearby return). These are discovered path families, not predefined one-step codes. | In a 44-reader Spanish sentence experiment, syntactic garden-path versus comparison sentences provide an independent **text manipulation**. Only cluster **A** was significantly more frequent in the garden-path condition; B and C were not. The finer D–Q solution was exploratory. | Supports studying whole-path rereading geometry and condition sensitivity. It does not ground a universal “confused” label for an isolated regression or a phone line cycle. |
| [Strukelj & Niehorster (2018)](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/download/Strukelj/Strukelj_final_pdf), methods, Fig. 2 and discussion | Eye movements were recorded during **instructed** regular reading, thorough reading, skimming, and spell checking of full pages. | Sixty analyzed readers used a 120 Hz desktop tracker. Instructions assign the task condition; comprehension questions and duration differences support that readers broadly complied. Thorough reading showed more rereading and **higher** comprehension; skimming had longer saccades, shorter fixations, more word skipping, and lower comprehension. | Stronger anchor for **task-dependent aggregate behavior**, including evidence that rereading is not intrinsically harmful. It does not publish an exact arrow-string classifier that identifies a reader's intention during ordinary phone reading. |
| [Parshina et al. (2022)](https://tmalsburg.github.io/ParshinaEtAl2022.pdf), Figs. 3–5 and results | Word-level whole-sentence paths were clustered into “fluent,” “intermediate,” and “beginner” **descriptive** processes in Russian readers. | Reader-language groups, heritage-language proficiency, and L2 comprehension scores were external comparisons. The authors explicitly describe a **continuum** summarized by the clusters, not three independently labeled states. | Useful exploratory precedent for whole-path analysis; the cluster names are not a NewsMead momentary ability or aid-need ground truth. |
| [GazePrompt (Wang et al., 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772), §3.2.3 and §4.3.2 | Its software recognized line following, switching, and jumping with layout-specific fixation and return-sweep rules. | A 13-reader low-vision desktop aid trial found shorter line-switching time with the aid, but no significant gain in line-switching accuracy. The authors describe a slow back-trace misidentified as a line jump; no independent event-class detector ground truth was reported. | Its rules and tested cue are relevant *implementation and intervention* precedents, not validated NewsMead pattern labels. |

## Status of the arrows shown in the NewsMead proposal

| Schematic order | Provenance | Defensible reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `w12 → w13`, `w12 → w12`, `w13 → w12`, `w12 → w15` | Direct applications of Olivier's `Fwd`, `Rfx`, `Bwd`, `Fwd+` **after reliable word assignment**. The numerals are illustrative, not copied participant traces. | Next word, same-word refixation, previous word, or farther forward landing, respectively. |
| `w12 → w13 → w12` | Composition of Olivier's `Fwd` then `Bwd`; no separately named published class. | Observed word return; no known reason for it. |
| `L4(end) → L5(start)` | Published return-sweep geometry in Yamaya and Parker. | Candidate next-line return sweep if the end/start geometry and line assignments are verified. `L4 → L5` alone establishes only adjacent assigned lines. |
| `L5 → L4 → L5` and `L4 → L5 → L4 → L5` | **NewsMead schematic compositions**, related to published earlier-line return/resumption movements. | Repeated assigned line visits. No cited paper validates these exact cycles as confusion, difficulty, or an aid trigger. |
| Long stay on `L4` | A duration measurement, **not an order pattern**. | Observed line residence only when coverage and text amount are known. No source above validates “long line stay → focus window helps.” |
| `w12 → [gap] → w16` | NewsMead continuity rule. | Unknown path during the gap; never a measured skip. |

**Implementation consequence:** first establish independently checked word/line assignments and event annotations on the target phone. Evaluate each observable class's precision, recall, coverage, and failure modes on readers and texts excluded from tuning. Test behavioral interpretations and visual-aid benefit against separate outcomes. The [first NewsMead natural-reading diagnostic](reading-measurement-run-01.md) had only four eligible word transitions after gap correction, so it cannot validate a word-order classifier yet.
