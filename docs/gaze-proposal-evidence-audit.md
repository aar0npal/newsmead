# Evidence audit of the ordered-line proposal

Prepared 2026-09-23 for the NewsMead thesis. This is a critical review of the [working line-visit proposal](scanpath-scaffolding-proposal.md). The [current adaptive-visual-aid proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md) responds to the project owner's clarification that support must adapt during ordinary reading. The earlier [interruption-bookmark option](evidence-backed-reading-support-proposal.md) is retained as a historical alternative. “Unsupported” below means **not yet validated for the intended NewsMead claim**; it does not mean the idea is useless. Novel design choices are acceptable research contributions when they are named as hypotheses and tested independently.

## Overall judgment

**Current-direction note:** The option ranking and interruption recommendation below preceded the project owner's clarification that the intended implementation runs from **ordered word and line scanpaths through observable pattern classification to adaptable visual aid** during ordinary reading. The [current implementation proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md) uses that architecture; it does not require a universal instability score, a supervised support-need model, or one fixed line-guide form. It includes word-level feasibility testing and treats a slow-progress-triggered focus window as an unvalidated intervention candidate. Keep this audit for its challenges to unsupported pattern meanings and pattern-to-aid rules, not as the current product recommendation.

The historical line-visit proposal was too assertive in selecting a **fixed pattern-to-aid controller** before NewsMead demonstrated reliable natural-reading line events or evidence that its proposed patterns identify a moment when assistance helps. Published work supports analyzing ordered gaze and recognizes return sweeps and rereading, but it does not validate NewsMead's line bins, cycling grammar, intervention rules, or a “reading instability” construct. The current diagnostic evidence cannot fill those gaps: one 22 dp visible-dot known-target run reported 61.6% guided exact-line assignment, and the first natural-reading diagnostic yielded only four eligible **word** transitions after gap correction; neither measured the proposed line-event precision in the target population. See [validation protocol](reading-validation-protocol.md) and [run 01](reading-measurement-run-01.md).

The defensible near-term path is an **interruption-and-place-finding study** with an externally known interruption and a simple location cue. It is directly motivated by reading-interruption experiments. Gaze can be used to locate the last reading area **only if local placement accuracy is demonstrated**. Automatic “difficulty” detection from spontaneous line cycling should remain an exploratory question, not the main thesis mechanism.

This recommendation **changes the thesis claim**. A known-interruption cue is event-triggered reading support, not proof of continuous detection of spontaneous “instability.” If continuous spontaneous adaptation is non-negotiable, the honest path is first to establish an independent need-for-help label and validate a phone detector against it. No cited study currently supplies a ready-to-use NewsMead rule. A new formula or alphabet cannot substitute for that evidence.

## Audit: claims and choices without direct validation

### Target, construct, and choice of representation

| Proposal choice or implied claim | What exists | Challenge and better treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adults aged **45+** have a particular need for this detector and cue. | This is the thesis' selected population. [Warrington et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1037/xlm0000543) found age and text-quality effects in young, middle-aged (41–51), and older (65+) readers. | Age effects on reading do not establish that this specific phone detector or cue helps everyone 45+. Keep 45+ as a justified **scope choice** and measure age, vision, digital-reading experience, and variation within the group. Do not label 45–59 as “older adults” in results. |
| “Reading stability/instability” is an observable momentary state that ordered line visits can detect. | [Olivier et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.15.4.5) modeled task-specific word-movement phases; [Yamaya et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.2197/ipsjjip.25.100) classified segments to improve text alignment. Neither validated NewsMead's latent state. | Define **observable navigation events** only. Test whether they predict an independently defined need or benefit before naming a cognitive state. A backward visit may be useful rereading; [Schotter et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614531148) experimentally showed comprehension can depend on regressions, and [Chevet et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101565) found more rereading after digital-reading interruptions without a comprehension loss in their task. |
| A deterministic **physical-line visit sequence** should be the primary control representation. | Literature provides word-level order models and multiline alignment methods. No comparison on NewsMead shows line visits outperform broader text regions, explicit interruption events, or user requests. | Retain line visits as a **candidate measurement**, not a chosen controller. Compare event/placement reliability with a broader region representation and an interruption-based anchor on development data. Select the least granular signal that meets the study's intended outcome. |
| No participant baseline or weights are needed. | A deterministic event detector does not mathematically need weights, but this does not establish that a universal duration or persistence rule works across readers. | If the claim is simply “the reader revisited a line,” no baseline is needed. If the claim is “this reader is unusually disrupted,” validate a reader-relative rule against independent labels; do not infer that removing weights solves construct validity. |
| A fitted HSMM should be an offline comparator. | Olivier's word-level HSMM is a valid methodological precedent in a topical-search task. | It adds model fitting and interpretive degrees of freedom without a defined NewsMead comparison question or adequate line-event data. Remove it from the core study; use only for a separately justified exploratory analysis with sufficient data and external validation. |

### Measurement and category choices

| Proposal choice or implied claim | What exists | Challenge and better treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone gaze can support **exact physical-line** visits during natural news reading. | One closed-loop known-target run at 22 dp reported 89.3% exact-line on word targets but **61.6% exact-line during guided-line reading**. Its 99.2% within-one-line score is a different, coarser endpoint. The first natural run reported vertical misalignment and too few eligible transitions. [Carr et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0) document line-assignment difficulty even in laboratory data. | This is a **blocking feasibility question** for exact-line cues. Repeated, independently labeled target-population trials must measure per-line confusion, lag, coverage, and false reversals in the final dot/calibration/layout condition. Neighbor-line agreement cannot be presented as exact-line success. |
| The visible orange dot, One Euro smoothing, and calibration produce a deployable observation stream. | The v6 check measured those factors together after recalibration. The first natural recording used raw coordinates for analysis while the displayed dot was smoothed. | Do a **matched** comparison of raw versus displayed coordinates on the same recorded sessions and explicitly test the final feedback condition. The dot may change gaze behavior; neither smoothing nor tracking eligibility proves correct line assignment. Do not pool v4/v5/v6. |
| A “stable event,” line visit, and quality gate can be defined with chosen dispersion, minimum duration, confidence, and gap limits. | Fixation algorithms have methodological precedent ([Salvucci & Goldberg, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/355017.355028)); NewsMead's thresholds are engineering choices. The 125 ms natural-run gap rule prevented false bridging but is not a validated physiological cutoff. | Freeze candidate rules using development data; report sensitivity, eligible time, and missed events against **independent annotations**. Quality gating may preferentially exclude hard episodes, so report abstentions and the excluded population as outcomes, not merely clean the data. |
| `FWD1`, `FWD_LONG`, `BWD1`, `BWD_LONG` are meaningful behavior classes. | [Parker et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2018.12.007) establish normal return sweeps; Olivier encoded signed **word** distance; Yamaya distinguished same-line and multiline rereading. | These are NewsMead's **line-ordinal bins**, not copied validated classes. A missing intermediate visit does not prove skipped reading; a backward line estimate can be tracker error; and “one versus many lines” has no demonstrated different psychological or intervention meaning. Keep signed line displacement as a continuous descriptive field first; bin only if reliability or decision value warrants it. |
| `HOLD` means prolonged residence and may contribute to a trigger. | Duration is measurable in principle, but a line's length, difficulty, font size, and deliberate rereading all affect time. Olivier's `Rfx` means returning to the **same word**, not a long line dwell. | Remove `HOLD` from any primary trigger. If retained for description, normalize by visible text/line length and analyze task context; validate it against an independent outcome. |
| `BREAK` can safely delimit the sequence. | Missingness handling is methodologically necessary; the first analyzer incorrectly bridged long unclassified intervals. | Keep `BREAK` as a **data-quality state**, not a reading state. Define how scrolling, text reflow, off-text gaze, and uncertain line mapping end a sequence; audit the rate and reasons for breaks. Do not treat post-break jumps as reader behavior. |
| Physical lines and their ordinals remain stable over scrolling and font changes. | Android text geometry is logged, but the proposed online line-visit extractor and its mapping across layout changes have not been validated. | Version the layout and text anchor; compare only visits with a defensible common document location. A line number alone changes when font size or width changes. |

### Detector and intervention choices

| Proposal choice or implied claim | What exists | Challenge and better treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `4 → 5 → 4 → 5`, extended revisits, and cycling are distinct signs worth detecting. | Ordered scanpaths reveal information lost in counts, but the exact NewsMead patterns and their base rates are untested. | Treat the examples as **descriptive test cases**, not a prespecified clinical or support grammar. First measure inter-annotator agreement and classifier precision/recall. Then test whether each pattern predicts an external need-for-help label or cue benefit beyond simple interruption/scroll events. |
| A return to a prior “frontier” means the reader has recovered. | There is no independent proof that revisiting a former line position restores understanding or place. | Report only that the observed line ordinal was reached. Do not use gaze-derived frontier return as the primary recovery outcome. |
| A single backward move should not trigger, while persistence should. | This is a sensible caution, but neither the number of moves nor persistence time has a validated cost-benefit threshold. | Estimate false cues and missed useful cues on development data; choose a rule using a predeclared acceptable error tradeoff, then freeze and test it on separate readers/passages. |
| Local revisit → subtle anchor; extended revisit → broad cue; cycling → mild cue; `BREAK` → hold/clear. | [Cane et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.656666) and [Jo et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702340) support location cues **after known interruptions**, in different populations and hardware. They do not validate these spontaneous-pattern-to-cue mappings. | Remove the mapping from the primary hypothesis. Test one cue at a time after an objectively known event. Compare placement and user benefit separately. An incorrect cue could direct attention to the wrong text. |
| Escalation, fade, cooldown, scroll suppression, and abstention will improve comfort or avoid harm. | No tested NewsMead timing or policy exists. | Treat each as interface parameters. Favor one fixed-duration cue in the first efficacy test; record unwanted cues and distraction. Add policy complexity only if the initial cue shows benefit and the new rule has a specific testable rationale. |
| Four levels, continuous intensity, or combined scaffolds should be available. | Four renderers exist in code; the thesis theory does not establish that four levels, or any graded policy, outperform a single cue. | Do not make level count a research premise. If the first cue works, later compare cue forms with enough participants; do not optimize many forms on the same evaluation sample. |
| Broader region cues or user-invoked support are fallback solutions. | Jo's block cue is a closer direct precedent than line-cycling scaffolds, but was tested with a Tobii X60 desktop tracker, young Korean readers, and controlled interruptions. | These are **better-supported candidates**, not established NewsMead solutions. A user-placed or user-requested marker reduces trigger ambiguity; a broad cue reduces exact-line placement demands. Both still need usability and effectiveness tests for adults 45+ on a phone. |

### Evaluation choices

| Proposal choice or implied claim | What exists | Challenge and better treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adaptive versus static reading isolates the benefit of **adaptation**. | Counterbalancing condition and article is a reasonable design feature. | If static has no cue, the contrast combines **having any cue** with **when/how it is triggered**. First establish cue versus no cue. A later comparison against the **same cue presented by a fixed or user-controlled rule** is needed to claim incremental benefit from gaze adaptation. |
| “Recovery latency” can be the primary outcome. | The thesis currently defines recovery as the **same index returning to baseline** after it triggers assistance (`chapter_3.tex`, `chapter_4.tex`). This is circular. [Cane et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.656666) and [Zermiani et al. (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.802/) study externally timed interruptions and annotate resumption separately. | Fix an external onset and an outcome independent of the detector. A timed **correct-line location response** is a practical objective endpoint, but call it *place-finding time*, not full cognitive recovery. To claim reading-resumption latency, use an independent reference or blinded human annotation of behavior plus comprehension safeguards. |
| Comprehension, speed, usability, and unwanted cues are adequate safeguards. | These are relevant outcomes; [Jo et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702340) reduced resumption lag in young readers but did **not** show a comprehension benefit. | Prespecify a primary endpoint, a tolerable comprehension-loss margin, and how missing gaze/cues are counted. Report uncertainty; a small study cannot establish non-harm merely because a comprehension test is non-significant. Do not infer comprehension gain from faster place finding. |
| About **15 participants** and a paired test can establish effectiveness. | The thesis has an approximate target of 15; no current effect-size/variance estimate, attrition model, or event-level power calculation is documented for the new outcome. | Treat 15 as a **feasibility/pilot size** unless a justified precision or power calculation supports a confirmatory claim. Multiple interruptions in one person are correlated and do not replace independent readers. Predefine analysis at reader level or with an appropriate repeated-measures model; avoid choosing a test solely from a Shapiro–Wilk result. |
| Development-set tuning followed by an independent evaluation is already secured. | The proposal recommends separation but the actual participants, passages, and analysis split are open. | Write the split, intervention, acceptable error criteria, and analysis **before** confirmatory collection. Do not tune after seeing the evaluation outcomes. |

## Better-supported options, ranked for this thesis

| Option | Direct research basis | Fit to NewsMead and remaining obstacle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Known interruption + one location cue** (recommended next study) | [Cane et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.656666) found a visual marker at the interruption location aided reading resumption. [EyeBookmark](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702340) compared gaze-based point, sentence, and block cues; block/sentence cues shortened resumption lag in controlled desktop studies. [InteRead](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.802/) provides an interrupted-reading dataset and separate resumption annotations. | Directly addresses the thesis' **reorientation** goal without labeling spontaneous rereading as difficulty. The interruption onset is known. The hard local question becomes cue-location accuracy and whether a phone cue helps the 45+ sample. The existing results do not transfer automatically. |
| **2. User-requested or user-placed place marker** | Same interruption-cue literature supports the function of a spatial marker, while user activation avoids an unvalidated automatic need detector. | Strong fallback or comparator if phone gaze cannot place a cue reliably. It tests reading support, but **cannot establish gaze-driven adaptation** by itself. |
| **3. Gaze-supported next-line cue** | [Parker et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2018.12.007) establish return sweeps; [GazePrompt](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642878) reported faster line switching with gaze-aware support in 13 low-vision readers. | More directly tied to line movement than cycling. Still requires reliable next-line detection on NewsMead and target-population testing; low-vision reading-aloud results are not 45+ silent news-reading results. |
| **4. Spontaneous line-pattern controller** | Olivier/Yamaya support sequence analysis, not NewsMead's help trigger. | Keep as exploratory until line-event measurement, pattern meaning, and cue benefit each pass independent tests. It is currently the least defensible **primary efficacy** route. |

## Evidence-gated replacement path

1. **State the narrow claim:** “A location cue helps adults aged 45+ find their place after a known interruption while reading news on a phone.” Do not claim it detects confusion or improves comprehension without separate evidence.
2. **Separate cue design from gaze sensing:** first compare a correctly placed cue with no cue under controlled interruptions. Verify the reader's location when the interruption begins; a participant-confirmed bookmark or controlled target can supply placement for this *cue-efficacy* phase. A timer alone gives an onset but not a true reading location. If a sufficiently precise study rules out a useful benefit or finds material distraction, stop developing an automatic controller around that cue. An inconclusive small pilot is not proof of no benefit.
3. **Validate phone placement independently:** in target-population sessions under the final font, dot, calibration, and posture, compare a gaze-inferred **region** with a known or independently marked location at interruption. Report exact/neighbor/broad-region confusion, latency, coverage, and abstention by reader; select cue granularity from these results rather than from one known-target accuracy percentage.
4. **Choose an independent endpoint:** if participants tap the line where they will resume, measure time to a **correct tap** from interruption offset and label it place-finding time. If the study needs true reading-resumption latency, arrange independent observation/annotation and state its reliability; the trigger's own gaze score cannot be both treatment rule and success definition. Add comprehension and unwanted-assistance outcomes.
5. **Only then test gaze automation:** compare a gaze-placed cue with a fair cue control in a separate, frozen evaluation. If the intended claim is about *adaptation*, the control must receive the same visual cue under a non-gaze rule; cue versus no cue alone supports only the presence-of-cue claim.
6. **Power and analysis:** estimate the reader-level variation and missingness from pilot data, specify a smallest useful effect/precision target, then justify sample size. Counterbalance article and condition, keep tuning readers/passages separate, and analyze correlated episodes within readers. At approximately 15 readers, report uncertainty and feasibility rather than treating a non-significant test as proof of no effect.

### Decision rule before implementation claims

Do not set a numerical “validated” accuracy or detector threshold by importing a value from another tracker or study. Prespecify what wrong cues cost the user and the **maximum tolerable false-placement/false-trigger rate** for this interface; estimate uncertainty in a development sample, freeze the rule, and evaluate it on independent participants/passages. Failure to meet that criterion should produce an explicit abstention or a user-requested cue, not a stronger inference from noisy gaze.

## Thesis changes required if this path is chosen

The current thesis says the Adaptive Instability Index is the trigger and the basis for its own recovery-latency outcome (`../../THS-ST-Paper/chapter_3.tex`, `../../THS-ST-Paper/chapter_4.tex`). It also claims that rereading time is extraneous load and that graded support is required. These are **not consequences established by the cited theories**: rereading can preserve comprehension, and a theory of scaffolding does not determine four display levels or an optimal controller. The thesis' general age-related eye-movement evidence also does not directly establish that adults 45+ have longer **interruption recovery** on NewsMead. Finally, the thesis argues that ten-second smoothing averages gaze error down; systematic line offsets and selective missingness do not vanish by averaging. These claims need narrower wording or new independent evidence. The old weighted index, the ordered-line detector, and the interruption cue are **different study mechanisms**; the manuscript must describe the one actually built and evaluated. No manuscript source was edited during this audit.

## Primary-source map and transfer limits

- [Cane, Cauchard, & Weger (2012), *The time-course of recovery from interruption during reading*](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.656666): two paragraph-reading experiments; location cue aided resumption after spoken-story interruption. Does not validate a phone gaze marker or adults 45+.
- [Jo, Kim, & Seo (2015), *EyeBookmark: Assisting Recovery from Interruption during Reading*](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702340): CHI study of gaze-based visual bookmarks. Main study had 13 Korean adults aged 20–28 using Tobii X60, a desktop screen, and chin rest; block/sentence cues reduced annotated resumption lag, with no significant comprehension difference. Does not validate NewsMead placement or population.
- [Zermiani et al. (2024), *InteRead*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.802/): 50-participant interrupted-reading dataset with annotated interruption/resumption events; methodological precedent, not a cue efficacy trial.
- [Wang et al. (2024), *GazePrompt*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642878): 13 low-vision readers in controlled reading-aloud tasks; line-switching aid shortened switching time. Different population, task, device, and aid.
- [Olivier et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.15.4.5), [Yamaya et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.2197/ipsjjip.25.100), and [Carr et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0): ordered word/segment measurement and multiline alignment precedents; none validates a spontaneous phone line-pattern support controller.
- [Schotter, Tran, & Rayner (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614531148): preventing rereading reduced comprehension; rereading should not be automatically classified as failure.
- [Chevet et al. (2022), *Effects of interruption on eye movements and comprehension during reading on digital devices*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101565): experimentally interrupted long-text reading, observed more rereading, and found no significant loss for locally interrupted information; higher comprehenders also reread more. The association does not prove every regression caused better comprehension.
- [Warrington et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1037/xlm0000543): reading and stimulus-quality effects across young, middle-aged, and older adults; motivates testing age and visual conditions, not assuming one cue effect across 45+.

This was a targeted source audit, not a systematic review. The research record remains open to better direct evidence and the local validation plan must be finalized before a confirmatory claim.
