# Evidence for the word-and-line scanpath proposal

Prepared 2026-09-23 as a **targeted primary-study review**, not a systematic or exhaustive literature review. This file records precedents and limits for the [current word-and-line scanpath-to-visual-aid implementation proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md) and the [historical fixed line-visit option](scanpath-scaffolding-proposal.md); read the [critical audit](gaze-proposal-evidence-audit.md) before treating particular patterns as aid triggers. Each citation was checked against a primary article, publisher record, or author manuscript. The teammate's Mahalanobis literature PDFs informed the question but are secondary compilations, not independent validation of the proposed controller.

## What the literature establishes

| Proposal claim | Evidence status | Required NewsMead check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visit order carries information lost in aggregate fixation/regression counts | **Direct reading-method evidence** from controlled laboratory scanpath studies | Reproduce useful ordered **word and line** events from NewsMead's phone stream; validate each resolution separately |
| A backward visit is an observable rereading pattern, not automatically a problem | **Direct experimental evidence** that rereading can aid comprehension; multiple purposes for regressions | Distinguish real returns from tracker error and ordinary line corrections; avoid negative labels |
| Duration-aware sequence models can represent different reading phases | **Direct method precedent** from a word-level, task-specific HSMM | Fit on NewsMead data if explored; verify states against external outcomes before interpretation |
| Smartphone gaze can support coarse reading analyses | **Adjacent device evidence** under another phone/model and controlled conditions | Measure local line/region accuracy, lag, continuity, and false transitions at each intended font size |
| Gaze-contingent reading aids can benefit readers | **Adjacent intervention evidence** in different populations and modalities | Test this visual scaffold policy against a matched static interface and independent outcomes |
| NewsMead's exact line-pattern triggers reduce recovery time or improve comprehension | **No direct published or local validation yet** | Prespecify and run an independent intervention evaluation |

The papers below should be cited for the narrow claim in the “Supports” column. None justifies calling NewsMead's deterministic patterns a validated detector of “reading instability,” confusion, or mind wandering. The word-level laboratory papers do not establish exact-line attribution on a phone.

## Provenance of the proposed order categories

The proposal's word-transition labels follow a **published operational alphabet** when consecutive fixations can both be assigned to words. [Olivier et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.15.4.5) encoded the signed number of **words crossed** between fixations as `Fwd+`, `Fwd`, `Rfx`, `Bwd`, and `Bwd-`. [Yamaya, Topić, and Aizawa (2017)](https://doi.org/10.2197/ipsjjip.25.100) classified transitions between *sequential reading segments* as Return Sweep, Progressing, Short Re-reading, Long Re-reading, Skipping, and Resuming. The latter study used a Tobii TX300 screen-based tracker, 11 readers and 33 English-document trials to improve fixation-to-word mapping. Its segment classes are **not** NewsMead's line-ordinal bins, and its 87% mapping accuracy is not phone word/line accuracy or scaffold validity. [Carr et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0) independently document within- and between-line regressions and errors in assigning fixations to lines.

NewsMead's **line** labels remain original operational definitions. Keep word-fixation order, word-visit duration, and line visits as related but distinct records. `Rfx` means consecutive fixations on one word; a later return after leaving that word is a revisit. `HOLD` is duration, not an Olivier order category. A missing fixation or word assignment cannot establish that text was skipped.

| NewsMead label | Relevant published observation | Exact transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FWD1` | A return sweep normally moves from one line's end to the next line's start (Yamaya; Parker et al. 2019). | Our label means the **next physical line in document order**, regardless of leftward screen movement. No cited paper validates its detection in NewsMead. |
| `FWD_LONG` | Olivier distinguishes forward movements by **word** distance; Yamaya includes a task-specific Skipping segment class. | Our label means only that the next eligible visit is over one physical line ahead. Missing visits cannot establish that the reader ignored or failed to process the intervening text. It replaces the earlier draft name `FWD_SKIP`. |
| `BWD1`, `BWD_LONG` | Olivier distinguishes backward movements by **word** distance; Carr describes between-line regressions; Yamaya's Long Re-reading crosses lines. | NewsMead's split is **one versus more than one physical line backward**. Yamaya's “short” versus “long” split means same-line versus multiline rereading, so it is not equivalent. Neither label proves confusion. |
| `HOLD` | Fixation/visit duration is a measured temporal property; Olivier's `Rfx` refers to the **same word** being fixated again. | `HOLD` is NewsMead's candidate line-residence condition, not Olivier's `Rfx` or an established difficulty class. |
| `BREAK` | Multiline gaze assignment can be wrong under vertical drift and unusual rereading (Carr). | This is NewsMead's missingness and continuity rule, **not** a reader-behavior category. It prevents false sequence claims across uncertain intervals. |

The defensible panel claim is that **ordered transitions, return sweeps, and between-line rereading have research precedent**. The proposed line bins and pattern-triggered assistance still need independent validation on the intended phone, layout, and participant population.

## Core primary studies

### Ordered scanpaths and context

| Study and primary link | Population, task, measurement | Supports | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| von der Malsburg & Vasishth (2011), [*What is the scanpath signature of syntactic reanalysis?*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2011.02.004) | Reanalysis of eye movements from 44 undergraduates reading Spanish sentences with syntactic ambiguity; compared ordered fixation paths. | Different rereading sequences can be distinguished when simple regression summaries miss them. | A sentence-level syntactic task with word fixations; no validated phone line grammar, scaffold trigger, or universal cognitive label. |
| von der Malsburg, Kliegl, & Vasishth (2015), [*Determinants of Scanpath Regularity in Reading*](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12208) | Potsdam corpus: 230 readers and 144 isolated German sentences; scanpath regularity related to age, word length, and syntactic factors. | Order-sensitive measurement captures reading-related variation and must be interpreted with reader/text context. | Greater irregularity with age or difficult text is not impairment or a momentary need for assistance; short lab sentences differ from scrolling news. |
| Parshina, Sekerina, Lopukhina, & von der Malsburg (2022), [*Monolingual and Bilingual Reading Processes in Russian: An Exploratory Scanpath Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.414) | Compared ordered eye movements of Russian-speaking adults, children, heritage readers, and second-language learners. | Recurring scanpath families described as fluent, intermediate, and beginner differed in pace, skipping, and rereading across these groups. | These are group- and language-linked interpretations from precise word-level paths, not conclusive labels for a NewsMead user's momentary state. |
| Mézière, Yu, McArthur, Reichle, & von der Malsburg (2024), [*Scanpath Regularity as an Index of Reading Comprehension*](https://doi.org/10.1080/10888438.2023.2232063) | Reanalysis of eye movements from comprehension tests with different task demands; compared scanpath measures with word-based measures. | Scanpath density/dissimilarity can add information about **between-reader** comprehension performance. | Best predictors and even directions varied by test; neither a single universal scanpath metric nor real-time individual scaffold triggers were established. |
| Olivier, Guérin-Dugué, & Durand (2022), [*Hidden Semi-Markov Models to Segment Reading Phases from Eye Movements*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10292930/) | Readers judged whether texts related to a target topic; a fitted HSMM segmented **word-level** eye-movement sequences into task phases. | A duration-aware sequential model can recover heterogeneous patterns and provides an offline comparison architecture. | States were interpreted from a particular information-seeking task, with large reader variability. Published parameters/labels do not transfer to NewsMead lines, and retrospective decoding is not live prediction. |
| Strukelj & Niehorster (2018), [*One page of text: Eye movements during regular and thorough reading, skimming, and spell checking*](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.11.1.1) | 60 retained young Swedish adults under four instructed reading goals; 120 Hz desktop eye tracker. | Different goals produce different fixation, skipping, rereading, and comprehension profiles; behavior must be interpreted in task context. | Patterns observed under explicit instructions are not conclusive labels for unprompted intent in older phone readers. |

### Rereading and line transitions

| Study and primary link | Population, task, measurement | Supports | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Booth & Weger (2013), [*The function of regressions in reading: backward eye movements allow rereading*](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-012-0244-y) | Two controlled experiments altered whether returning eyes could retrieve earlier visual text. | Backward eye movements can serve actual rereading rather than merely a spatial memory pointer. | A return is a behavior, not a diagnosis of confusion or failed reading. |
| Schotter, Tran, & Rayner (2014), [*Don't Believe What You Read (Only Once): Comprehension Is Supported by Regressions During Reading*](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614531148) | Trailing-mask experiment compared normal reading with text that became unavailable after the eyes passed it. | Functionally preventing rereading reduced comprehension; fewer regressions cannot be assumed beneficial. | Text masking is not NewsMead's scaffold; it does not identify which spontaneous returns need help. |
| Chevet et al. (2022), [*Effects of interruption on eye movements and comprehension during reading on digital devices*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101565) | Interrupted reading of long digital texts with an arithmetic task; examined eye movements and questions about interrupted information. | Rereading increased after interruption; comprehension of locally interrupted information was not significantly lower, and higher comprehenders reread more in this sample. | The link between rereading and comprehension is observational within this experiment; it does not show that every reread is needed or that a NewsMead cue improves outcomes. |
| Parker, Slattery, & Kirkby (2019), [*Return-sweep saccades during reading in adults and children*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2018.12.007) | Laboratory multiline reading by adults and children; examined line-end to next-line movements and corrections. | Return sweeps can undershoot and need corrective movements, so screen-left movement alone is not a regression. | Oculomotor corrections observed with precise tracking do not supply a phone-grade threshold or line-attribution accuracy. |
| Yamaya, Topić, & Aizawa (2017), [*Vertical Error Correction Using Classification of Transitions between Sequential Reading Segments*](https://doi.org/10.2197/ipsjjip.25.100) | 11 readers, 33 English-document trials with a Tobii TX300; 1,606 segment transitions manually labeled in six classes, then classified with a random forest. | Direct precedent for classifying **ordered transitions between multiline reading segments** to improve gaze-to-text alignment; the study reported 87% fixation-to-word mapping accuracy after its classification and alignment pipeline. | Its six classes, supervised model, eye tracker, and mapping objective differ from NewsMead's deterministic physical-line bins and scaffold triggers. Short Re-reading had F1 0.47 and Skipping F1 0.31, showing that the harder distinctions were not reliably solved even there. |
| Carr, Pescuma, Furlan, Ktori, & Crepaldi (2022), [*Algorithms for the automated correction of vertical drift in eye-tracking data*](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-021-01554-0) | Compared ten post-hoc line-assignment methods on simulated and natural multiline-reading eye-tracking data. | Documents vertical drift and within- versus between-line regressions; ordered gaze information can help line assignment, but returns can confound methods that assume strict progression. | It evaluates retrospective line assignment, not phone accuracy, the proposed category thresholds, or intervention benefit. |
| Tighe et al. (2023), [*Do Struggling Adult Readers Monitor Their Reading? Understanding the Role of Online and Offline Comprehension Monitoring Processes During Reading*](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194221081473) | Struggling adult readers performed comprehension-monitoring tasks with eye movements and later verbal explanations. | Longer regression-path duration related to better answers/explanations in this sample, reinforcing that rereading can be productive. | Association in a specific population/task does not prove a causal benefit from every regression or transfer to adults 45+. |

### Phone measurement and intervention precedent

| Study and primary link | Population, task, measurement | Supports | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valliappan et al. (2020), [*Accelerating eye movement research via accurate and affordable smartphone eye tracking*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18360-5) | Front-camera Pixel 2 XL at 30 Hz; a reading substudy had 17 usable participants reading scrolling SAT-like passages and answering factual/interpretive questions. | Calibrated phone gaze can support coarse spatial reading analyses and show task-related differences. | Different model/device and selected lab users; the authors caution that 30 Hz limits precise fixation duration, saccade latency, and velocity. It does not validate NewsMead exact-line assignment. |
| Wang et al. (2024), [*GazePrompt: Enhancing Low Vision People's Reading Experience with Gaze-Aware Augmentations*](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12772) | CHI study with 13 low-vision participants reading aloud using a 120 Hz desktop tracker; a second 13-person silent-reading study (with some participant overlap) was qualitative. The system offered line highlight/arrow and word magnifier/speech. | Direct architecture and aid precedent. Line-switching support reduced switching time (p = .014); switching accuracy did not significantly improve. Word support used prolonged first fixation, refixations, or one-pass dwell as triggers; neither maximum first-pass word fixation time nor misread-word count improved significantly. | Low-vision population, enlarged desktop text, and task differ from NewsMead's silent phone news reading. The study does not prove that long dwell identifies difficulty, that a word magnifier helps here, or that stronger cues help repeated line revisits. Most participants lengthened word-trigger thresholds to reduce false positives. |
| Cheong et al. (2009), [*Does a line guide improve reading performance with a stand magnifier?*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19661837/) | 29 people with age-related macular degeneration used a physical line guide with habitual stand magnification. | Some vertical-control behavior improved, while corrective movements during retracing rose and reading speed fell slightly; preference was mixed. | A physical guide is not a gaze-triggered phone overlay. It demonstrates a possible tradeoff, not a NewsMead aid rule. |
| Rayner, Slattery, & Bélanger (2010), [*Eye movements, the perceptual span, and reading speed*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3075059/) | 32 undergraduates with an EyeLink 1000 read under 1-, 2-, and 3-word moving windows and no window; fast and slow readers were compared. | Available parafoveal text affected reading rate, and the span/read-rate relationship differed between the groups. | The moving window was an experimental restriction, not evidence that a gaze-centered focus window aids a slow NewsMead reader. |
| Paterson et al. (2013), [*Filtered text reveals adult age differences in reading*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23066801/) | Young adults (18–30) and older adults (65+) read with a gaze-contingent normally displayed center and spatial-frequency-filtered peripheral text. | Peripheral text quality matters in reading and changes with age. | Filtering is not the same as NewsMead dimming, and this is a perceptual-span measurement, not a test of slow-progress-triggered focus support. |
| Scheiter et al. (2019), [*Adaptive multimedia: Using gaze-contingent instructional guidance to provide personalized processing support*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2019.05.005) | Three experiments with illustrated learning material (N = 32, 79, 62); a gaze-adaptive display was compared with static material. | Real-time gaze-conditioned visual adaptation can be implemented and evaluated independently; results were mixed, with no learning difference in Experiment 2 and harm to weaker-prerequisite learners in Experiment 3. | Different task, display, population, and policy. It does not support NewsMead's scanpath classes or strength mapping and cautions against assuming adaptation helps. |
| Cane, Cauchard, & Weger (2012), [*The time-course of recovery from interruption during reading*](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.656666) | Two experiments interrupted paragraph reading with a spoken news story while tracking eyes. | A visual cue at the interruption location substantially aided reading resumption in Experiment 2. | Known external interruptions, laboratory tracking and a visual cue; no validation of spontaneous difficulty detection, phone cue placement, or the NewsMead 45+ population. |
| Jo, Kim, & Seo (2015), [*EyeBookmark: Assisting Recovery from Interruption during Reading*](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702340) | CHI proof-of-concept: 13 young Korean adults in the main study, Tobii X60 on desktop with chin rest, interrupted paragraph reading and gaze-based location cues. | Block and sentence highlighting shortened manually measured resumption lag relative to no cue. | No significant comprehension improvement; accurate desktop gaze and known interruptions do not validate phone placement or automatic line-pattern triggers. |
| Zermiani et al. (2024), [*InteRead: An Eye Tracking Dataset of Interrupted Reading*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.802/) | 50-participant self-paced reading dataset with predefined interruptions and resumption-lag annotations. | Primary methodological precedent for recording a known interruption onset and annotating a resumption outcome separately from a proposed NewsMead trigger. | Dataset and analysis, not a NewsMead cue trial; its eye-tracking hardware and task do not set phone detector thresholds. |
| Schiavo et al. (2021), [*Attention-driven read-aloud technology increases reading comprehension in children with reading disabilities*](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12530) | Controlled comparison of gaze-paced read-aloud with traditional read-aloud in 20 children with dyslexia and 20 typical-reader controls. | Gaze-contingent assistance can be evaluated against **comprehension**, an outcome distinct from the trigger; the dyslexic group improved in that intervention. | Children, dyslexia, and audio pacing are not NewsMead's target population or visual policy. |
| Southwell et al. (2020), [*What Eye Movements Reveal About Later Comprehension of Long Connected Texts*](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12905) | Three reading studies (N = 104, 130, 147); later comprehension questions; participant-level cross-validation of global eye-movement features. | Demonstrates how to test gaze measures against an external reading outcome and separate readers during evaluation. | Passage/session-level prediction is not an instantaneous scaffold trigger; associations may change with text or task. |

### Measurement method, not behavioral validation

Salvucci & Goldberg (2000), [*Identifying Fixations and Saccades in Eye-Tracking Protocols*](https://doi.org/10.1145/355017.355028), classifies and compares fixation-identification methods using spatial and temporal information. It is a method precedent for a reproducible detector, **not** evidence that NewsMead's current I-DT parameters, ~17 Hz natural-reading recording, or line visits correspond to accurate physiological fixations. See [the pilot specification](reading-measurement-pilot.md) for local operational definitions and parameter caveats.

## What remains to validate locally

1. **Measurement:** target-population exact-word and exact-line confusion, eligible transition coverage at both resolutions, false backward jumps, latency, scroll/gap behavior, font-size effects, and repeatability on the intended phone and feedback presentation.
2. **Detector:** agreement with independent event annotations, false and missed pattern rates, threshold sensitivity, and stability across participants/passages. Use development data for tuning and separate evaluation data for the claimed result.
3. **Intervention:** compare matched adaptive/static conditions using an outcome whose event onset and success criterion are not defined by the detector's own score. Report comprehension, distraction, unwanted cues, and missingness alongside timing. Do not treat fewer triggers or faster score normalization as proof of improved reading.

The teammate's Mahalanobis literature matrix explicitly found no paper that validates its proposed three-feature distance as reading instability. Covariance-aware distance can describe atypicality; it cannot assign the meaning or benefit of an episode. This is why the working proposal uses descriptive sequence events first and reserves statistical summary models for comparison after measurement validation.

The [critical audit](gaze-proposal-evidence-audit.md) initially found a more direct literature route through known-interruption cues. After the project owner clarified that the primary aid must adapt during ordinary reading **and that the scanpath-to-aid system is the intended implementation**, the [current proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md) makes word and line scanpath derivation and descriptive pattern classification the central architecture. Word-level viability and specific pattern-to-aid mappings remain experimental until their measurement and benefit are independently established.
