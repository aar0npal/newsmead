# Public gaze-sequence models for reading capability: NewsMead evidence map

**Checked:** 2026-09-24. **Status:** research survey, not a model selection or deployment claim. **Scope:** publicly inspectable research implementations that infer a reader outcome from gaze during reading. Here, *capability* means a separately assessed reader property; passage comprehension, perceived difficulty, dyslexia status, and reading strategy are distinct outcomes.

## Main finding

[EyeBench (Shubi et al., NeurIPS 2025)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a3080b8e00bed560c433100a33ca6b2d-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) is the strongest public starting point for **comparing** methods. Its [MIT-licensed code](https://github.com/EyeBench/eyebench) harmonizes passage-level gaze and text, implements recurrent, convolutional, transformer, and conventional models, and tests on unseen readers, unseen texts, and both. It includes reader-level skill targets and passage-level outcomes. The authors explicitly report that no model consistently dominates and that current performance is not ready for real-world applications. Public training code and benchmark results are available; I did not verify downloadable, task-ready trained weights. The underlying datasets retain their own licenses.

EyeBench's included recordings used EyeLink or Portable Duo trackers at **1,000 Hz or higher**, with fixation-to-text alignment. Its published results therefore do not validate NewsMead's phone gaze stream, adults aged 45+, partial scanpaths, or aid decisions. The [NewsMead pilot](reading-measurement-pilot.md) measured a 58.63 ms median sample interval in one natural-reading record (about 17 Hz), with substantial uncertain word assignment; this is a specific pilot observation, not a device-wide rate.

## Evidence closer to NewsMead's camera hardware

The literature search does **not** require a news article: reading any text **on a phone** can inform hardware feasibility. No verified public pretrained model meets all four requirements: **ordinary phone front camera, older target readers, a validated reading-difficulty or capability target, and an online decision rule shown to choose aid type or strength**. Similar sampling frequency alone does not establish similar gaze accuracy or transferability. These studies are the closest relevant *research precedents*, with different outcomes and public assets:

| Study and availability | Hardware, population, and measured result | What NewsMead can take from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Lepton (Guo & Wang, ICMI 2018)](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/4596.pdf)**. No public model implementation or trained weights verified. | Google Nexus 5X front camera at **20 frames/s**, portrait mobile news reading, **25 participants aged 19–35**. Its periodic horizontal gaze/return-sweep method estimated lines read with **3.56 lines mean absolute error per page** in a task that prompted normal reading, rereading, or skipping. It detected those prompted page actions with reread/skip AUC **0.73/0.88**. It did not establish exact-line targeting or spontaneous difficulty recognition. | Best **phone-hardware and line-pattern precedent**. Reimplement and test a line/return-sweep comparator against NewsMead's independently annotated phone data if useful; the paper does not supply a plug-in capability model. The abstract's comprehension correlation **0.36 versus 0.29** must not be credited to gaze: Table 7 attributes **0.36** to traditional **plus scrolling** features, while gaze-only was **0.05**, traditional plus gaze **0.25**, and all three **0.31** in leave-one-reader-out evaluation. The authors also report their reproduction of SwitchBack's line count error rose from **3.9% to 20%** with a changed phone layout, a direct warning against copying thresholds. |
| **[StrategicReading (Guo, Cho & Wang, ICMI 2020)](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/5834.pdf)**. No public code or trained weights verified. | Google Nexus 5X portrait phone, front camera at **20 gaze estimates/s**, with scrolling and cross-page search/log data. **40 university readers aged 18–25** searched and read open-web sources on a phone. Models predicted later coded strategy scores from retrospective verbal reports and post-reading information gain and critical-question quality; a combined feature model reduced strategy-score RMSE by **17.79% on average** against constant-score baselines in leave-one-reader-out evaluation. | Strongest **phone precedent beyond article reading** for relating coarse line patterns to independently coded reading processes. Its labels are task-wide strategic processes, and its “reading paths” include page visits, not word-by-word scanpaths. The result does not supply a moment-by-moment difficulty label, an aid policy, or a deployable model. |
| **[Valliappan et al., *Nature Communications* 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18360-5)**. Gaze-estimator code was not released; its trained base estimator binary was available to academics on request, not as a public reading-difficulty model. | Pixel 2 XL Android front camera at **30 Hz**, with a personalized convolutional gaze estimator. In its reading sub-study, **17 retained adults** read SAT-like and computer-science passages while scrolling and answered factual/interpretive questions. Across **10 tasks**, fixation count correlated with *task-level* percentage incorrect (**r = 0.67**), and gaze time on a known answer-relevant excerpt correlated negatively (**r = −0.72**). The authors processed the study gaze offline. | Direct **phone gaze and scrolling evidence**. The published reading result is an association across tasks; there was **no trained per-reader or live difficulty classifier**. Its calibrated tracker accuracy cannot be presumed for NewsMead's MGazeNet, current phone posture, or older readers. |
| **[WebQAmGaze (Ribeiro et al., 2023 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17876)**; [public dataset and analysis code](https://github.com/tfnribeiro/WebQAmGaze). | Desktop/laptop webcams with WebGazer, mean **25.39 Hz**; **353 retained** of 600 recruited participants, mean age **29.47**. Ordinary and information-seeking reading with question outcomes. For ordinary reading, a gaze-feature random forest had **weighted F1 0.53 versus 0.51 random** among retained participants. The better information-seeking result (F1 **0.68**) reflects a different, question-directed task. Trials had a shuffled 80/20 split, not a held-out-reader split. | Best **open low-cost-camera data and code** for investigating quality filtering, regions, and baseline methods. Its published classifiers aggregate gaze features rather than learn ordered scanpaths. Neither the ordinary-reading result nor its split validates a phone aid controller on unseen older readers. |
| **[Hutt et al., *Behavior Research Methods* 2024](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BRMWebcamGaze.pdf)**. No public trained model/code verified. | Desktop webcam/WebGazer; **278 people** across two studies read or heard three-sentence stories with answer choices continuously visible. Coarse screen-region features predicted a later correct inference at **Cohen's κ 0.57** in reader-separated folds; the paper avoided fixation duration and saccade extraction because sampling was variable. The strongest regions were the positions of the three *answer choices*, a task layout NewsMead does not have during ordinary article reading. | Supports trying **coarse regions when point gaze is noisy**, with a separately evaluated outcome. It is not evidence that an ordered scanpath or an aid-need trigger works on news articles. |
| **[GazeReader (Ding et al., CHI EA 2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10443)**. No public trained gaze model/code verified. | Webcam gaze resampled to **20 Hz** from **12 graduate ESL learners**; an LSTM with RoBERTa and word features predicted **self-reported unknown words**, F1 **75.73%** on its main test and **56.31%** with held-out documents. Removing gaze changed F1 only from **75.73% to 75.59%** in the reported ablation. | A narrowly relevant **word-level aid idea**, not evidence that the gaze stream identifies unknown words on NewsMead's phone or that the model predicts general reading capability. |

These studies test different labels. A line return sweep describes navigation; an incorrect answer is a later outcome; an unknown word is a vocabulary report. None establishes that a long stay or regression means the reader needs a focus window, highlight, magnifier, or stronger aid.

## What the public models actually predict

| Candidate and public asset | Input and prediction target | Primary evidence | NewsMead interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EyeBench CopCo skill task:** random forest, AhnCNN/RNN, BEyeLSTM, RoBERTEye and other baselines; [code](https://github.com/EyeBench/eyebench) | One Danish passage's text-aligned gaze predicts the reader's **independent standardized comprehension score (1–10)**. CopCo has 57 readers in this benchmark. The random forest uses derived gaze features; it does **not** preserve the ordered fixation path as a sequence model does. | On [held-out readers](https://eyebench.github.io/eyebench/results/copco_rcs/), random-forest RMSE was **2.76** versus **2.66** for the constant baseline; BEyeLSTM was **2.64**. Across held-out texts for previously seen readers, forest RMSE was **2.22** versus **2.67**. The uneven result matters more than the average rank. [Task definition and data](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a3080b8e00bed560c433100a33ca6b2d-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf). | Closest **outcome definition** to general reading capability. Evidence does not support accurate assessment of a new NewsMead reader or a live cue trigger. Compare feature baselines with genuine sequence models rather than assuming a neural model is superior. |
| **EyeBench MECO L2 vocabulary task:** regression, random forest, RoBERTEye; [code](https://github.com/EyeBench/eyebench) | Gaze during English L2 reading predicts an independent LexTALE vocabulary score (0–100); 1,098 L2 readers in the harmonized benchmark. | [Held-out reader results](https://eyebench.github.io/eyebench/results/mecol2_lex/): forest RMSE **10.20** versus constant baseline **12.47**; RoBERTEye-W **11.11**. [Task definition](https://github.com/EyeBench/eyebench). | Demonstrates prediction of one specific reader property, vocabulary knowledge. It does not measure comprehension or momentary need for help among older news readers. |
| **BEyeLSTM (Reich et al., ETRA 2022):** [code](https://github.com/aeye-lab/etra-reading-comprehension) | LSTM over fixation-order gaze with global and linguistic features. SB-SAT: 95 readers, four SAT passages. Predicts comprehension-related outcomes and reader-rated passage difficulty. The dataset's general comprehension proxy is derived from answers on those passages, not a separate standardized test. | [Original study and reproducibility code](https://github.com/aeye-lab/etra-reading-comprehension); [EyeBench re-evaluation of passage difficulty](https://eyebench.github.io/eyebench/results/sbsat_std/) shows RMSE **0.67** for unseen readers versus **0.71** constant baseline, but **1.61** for unseen readers and texts versus **0.71** baseline. | A relevant **ordered-scanpath architecture** and a possible offline baseline. Its difficulty label is retrospective and passage-wide; the re-evaluation does not support an online difficulty controller. |
| **RoBERTEye / QEye family (Shubi et al., EMNLP 2024):** [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.198/), [MIT-licensed code](https://github.com/lacclab/Reading-Comprehension-Prediction) | Fixation- or word-level gaze combined with passage and question text predicts whether one comprehension answer will be correct. EyeBench's ordinary-reading OneStop subset has 180 adults reading English news passages. | The [original paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.198/) reports that this is difficult and gaze gains over a strong text-only model are generally small. In [EyeBench's later standardized test](https://eyebench.github.io/eyebench/results/onestop_rc/), held-out-reader-and-text AUROC was **0.572** for RoBERTEye-W, **0.550** for text-only RoBERTa; balanced accuracy was **54.1%** versus **53.7%**. | Closest **reading material** to NewsMead. Predicts a particular answer, not general capability or which visual aid helps. Models that receive the question text also need that input; a spontaneous aid before a question exists is a different task. |
| **Haller et al. (TSAR 2022):** bidirectional LSTM/CNN; [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tsar-1.10/), [code](https://github.com/hallerp/dyslexia-seqmod) | Word-order sequence of gaze-derived reading-measure vectors predicts dyslexia group in 62 Mandarin-speaking children reading sentences aloud. **This is a sequence over the words in the sentence, not the raw temporal order of individual fixations.** | In reader-separated nested 10-fold evaluation, subject-level LSTM AUROC **0.91** and aggregated SVM baseline **0.93**; authors found no advantage from the sequence models. The clinical group label came from a character-recognition test. [Results and methods](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tsar-1.10.pdf). | A public capability-related classifier, but its child population, Mandarin script, oral reading, and clinical target make it a poor direct NewsMead choice. It cannot be repurposed to diagnose ordinary reading difficulty. |
| **Olivier et al. (JEMR 2022):** HSMM; [paper and code links](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.15.4.5) | Word-to-word fixation movement symbols and state durations segment a completed scanpath into interpreted reading phases during information seeking. | 15 young adults on a 1,000 Hz EyeLink. Latent phases were interpreted post hoc; no independent reading-capability target or intervention validation. | A useful offline pattern-discovery example. It does not diagnose reader skill, momentary difficulty, or visual-aid need. |

**Weights versus code:** The linked repositories expose code or benchmark implementations. I found no verified downloadable task-specific checkpoints for these candidate predictors. This is an availability check, not a claim that weights could not be trained. A pretrained text encoder inside a gaze model would not make the gaze-outcome head a validated pretrained NewsMead model. Li et al.'s [2024 reading-ability LSTM preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08798) says code would be released later, but the [named repository](https://github.com/pumpkinLNX/LSTM-eye-tracking-pytorch) returned 404 at this check, so it does not meet the public-implementation criterion.

Other public gaze models can have weights while solving a different problem. [ScanDL 2.0](https://github.com/DiLi-Lab/ScanDL-2.0) releases pretrained weights to **generate synthetic reading scanpaths**; it does not infer a reader's capability from an observed scanpath. [Reading Recognition in the Wild](https://github.com/facebookresearch/reading_in_the_wild) detects whether someone is reading from glasses-based video, gaze, and head pose; it also does not score comprehension or need for help.

## Decision for the implementation proposal

1. **Keep the current [NewsMead scanpath-to-aid proposal](adaptive-visual-aid-proposal.md) as a descriptive measurement and intervention study.** No public predictor above is a validated map from a word/line pattern to *aid needed*, *aid type*, or *aid strength*. General capability is a reader property; difficulty and benefit can change by passage and situation.
2. **Use EyeBench as an offline research benchmark, if supervised prediction is added.** Compare a constant or text-only baseline, derived-gaze random forest, and one ordered-fixation model such as BEyeLSTM or RoBERTEye on the same prespecified outcome. Keep the forest's aggregate input separate from the models that preserve sequence order. Do not choose an architecture from its name alone.
3. **Establish NewsMead measurement first.** Validate candidate fixations, word assignment, line assignment, gaps, and usable coverage against independent annotations on the target phone and reading setup. Compare a Lepton-style line/return-sweep detector offline with the existing line-assignment method if the gaze stream supports it. Keep the word-level scanpath as a feasibility track. This comparator is a proposed implementation experiment, not a copied validated model; published Lepton thresholds and outcomes do not transfer automatically.
4. **Choose an independent label and validation split.** If assessing *general capability*, use a separately administered reading assessment. If assessing *passage comprehension*, use held-out question outcomes and compare against text-only prediction. If studying *aid need*, measure the effect of an aid against no aid or another aid; inferred phase, slow pace, or question error alone is not that label. Hold out readers and news passages as required by the intended use and keep model tuning separate from evaluation.
5. **Treat real-time use as a later, separate experiment.** The cited models mainly consume whole-passage data. Live adaptation requires causal partial-path inputs, latency and abstention rules, and an independent test that the resulting cues improve reading outcomes in NewsMead's target population.

No Android code or model weights were added as a result of this survey.
